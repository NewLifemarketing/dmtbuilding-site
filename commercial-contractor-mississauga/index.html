Two buildings, same size, different paths

Picture two identical warehouses on Employment-zoned land in Mississauga. One sits in the middle of an industrial block. The other backs onto a row of houses. Hiring a commercial contractor Mississauga owners trust starts with knowing that the City's Site Plan Control By-law 0293-2006 treats those two buildings differently, and that the difference has nothing to do with the building itself.

The by-law begins by designating all land in the City of Mississauga as a site plan control area. It then exempts lands with an Employment Zone. Then, in a later section, it pulls specific Employment lands back in. The second warehouse, close to residential zoning, is one of them. The first is not.

We are DMT Building Group, an ICF and concrete contractor based in Barrie, Ontario, building across the Greater Toronto Area. On commercial work we deliver engineered footings, foundations and insulated concrete form walls, as the structural contractor under your general contractor or carried through to the walls above grade. Which approvals your Mississauga project needs first is the question that sets our start date, so it is where this page starts too.

When Employment land in Mississauga still needs site plan approval

From section 6 of the consolidated by-law, updated May 1, 2024. Each of these brings site plan control back to Employment-zoned land.

  • Within 60 m of residential zoning. Employment lands within 60 m of lands zoned residential, where not already caught by another section.
  • Beside the green system. Employment lands that abut or include lands zoned greenlands, open space, parkway belt or the greenlands overlay.
  • On scheduled roads. Employment lands that abut the roads shown on the by-law's Schedules 1 and 2.
  • Particular sites and uses. Lands zoned E3-15, lands on several other schedules, all power generating facilities, and buildings over 15 m² on Utility-zoned land.

The practical point for anyone comparing commercial construction companies Mississauga has to offer: ask early which side of that line your parcel falls on. The City's building permit page lists site plan approval among the things you may need before you apply for a permit at all. That one fact can move a pour date by months, and any commercial construction company Mississauga developers bring in should be able to answer it before quoting.

Industrial or not: the City decides by use, not by zone

The second Mississauga-specific question is money. Development charges are paid before the building permit is issued, and for a non-residential building the City of Mississauga and the Region of Peel determine whether the industrial or the non-industrial rate applies. The City is explicit that zoning does not settle it. Its development charges page names restaurants, offices, banquet halls and commercial schools as uses that are not defined as industrial, even when they sit on Employment land.

That matters most on a speculative building, where nobody yet knows who the tenant will be. Mississauga lets the owner pay at the industrial rate during construction, with a letter of credit covering the difference between the two rates held for up to 36 months while the building waits for occupancy. When a tenant moves in, the City and the Region decide which charge applies. If you are redeveloping, two credits are worth asking about: an industrial expansion credit for existing industrial buildings, and a demolition credit where non-residential space is demolished and replaced, subject to the time limits in the by-laws.

None of this is our decision, and we do not price it. We raise it because it changes what is worth building. Where the charge and the use are settled early, the structural package can be designed once, for the building that will actually be occupied.

What a Mississauga non-residential permit asks of the structure

Every application goes in through ePlans. The City's checklist for a new non-residential building or addition is longer than the residential one, and several items land squarely on the concrete package:

  • Structural drawings with design criteria, construction details and specifications, not just layouts.
  • A Commitment to General Review form from the design professionals.
  • An SB-10 ASHRAE form, the energy side of the application, and the place a continuous-insulation wall shows up on paper.
  • A Letter of Use and a Supplementary Information Form. The use described here feeds straight back into the development charge question above.
  • An OBC matrix on the site plan, plus sprinkler drawings with hydraulic calculations and water supply flow test data.
  • The Applicable Law Form, which is where site plan approval, conservation authority permits and heritage clearances surface.

We are not the designer of record on these packages. Our part is constructability input on the ICF details before the drawings are finished, then building exactly to the engineer's stamp and flagging anything on site that does not match.

Creek valleys and the lake: conservation review in Mississauga

The green-system trigger in the site plan by-law has a partner outside City Hall. Most of Mississauga lies in the Credit River watershed, and the City is a member municipality of Credit Valley Conservation. CVC regulates construction of any building or structure, site grading, and placing or removing fill near a watercourse, floodplain, valley slope, wetland, the Lake Ontario shoreline or hazardous lands. Its permits are in addition to the City's, and its board approved updated regulation mapping on February 20, 2026, so check a site against the current map rather than an older one.

On the east side, Etobicoke Creek and Mimico Creek run through Mississauga on their way to Lake Ontario, and those watersheds belong to the Toronto and Region Conservation Authority. TRCA notes that a third of the Etobicoke Creek watershed lies in Mississauga. An industrial lot along either creek may answer to TRCA rather than CVC.

For the concrete, the overlap is practical. A site near a valley is often the same site that triggers site plan control, carries a grading plan with tight elevations, and needs its footings designed around soil that is not uniform. Those are the sites builders tend to call us about.

Commercial Contractor Mississauga — FAQs

Does an industrial building in Mississauga need site plan approval?

Not always. The Site Plan Control By-law exempts lands with an Employment Zone, but brings site plan control back for Employment lands within 60 m of residential zoning, abutting greenlands, open space or parkway belt, or abutting roads on Schedules 1 and 2, among others.

When are development charges paid in Mississauga?

Before the building permit is issued. The City and the Region of Peel decide whether the industrial or non-industrial rate applies.

Is an office on Employment land charged as industrial?

No. The City lists offices, restaurants, banquet halls and commercial schools as uses that are not defined as industrial in the development charges by-laws, regardless of zoning.

What if we do not have a tenant yet?

For a speculative building, the City allows payment at the industrial rate, with a letter of credit for the difference held for up to 36 months until the building is occupied.

Will we need a conservation authority permit?

Possibly. Credit Valley Conservation regulates most of Mississauga and TRCA regulates the Etobicoke and Mimico creek watersheds. Either may require a permit near a watercourse, valley, wetland or the lakeshore.

Are you based in Mississauga?

No. We are based in Barrie and build across the Greater Toronto Area, including Mississauga.
