The City's definition is wider than most people expect

Ask ten Barrie homeowners whether a sunroom on a slab or a second storey over the garage counts as an addition and you will get a few different answers. The City of Barrie gives only one. Its Building Services quick-start guide defines an addition as construction that increases the area of a building, whether by footprint or by adding storeys, and it says, without exceptions, that all additions require a building permit.

That one sentence settles a lot. Going up counts the same as going out. A small bump-out counts. A garage tied into the house counts. If you are weighing home additions Barrie side by side with buying something bigger, the permit is not a maybe; it is the first step of every version of the project.

We are DMT Building Group, based on High Street in Barrie. We build additions in insulated concrete form (ICF) and handle the excavation, footings, foundation and walls ourselves. Most of what follows is the part of the job that happens before any of that: the questions the City asks of a Barrie addition, and how to have the answers ready.

Five things that hold up a Barrie addition

Each of these comes straight from the City of Barrie's "Building an Addition" quick-start guide. None of them is unusual on the house additions Barrie reviews. All of them are cheaper to find at the kitchen table than at the permit desk.

  • A private septic system. If the house is not on municipal sewer, the City requires the existing septic system to be reviewed by a qualified designer to confirm it complies with the Ontario Building Code. More bedrooms or more floor area can change what the system has to handle.
  • Charges due before the permit. Development and education charges, parkland dedication and a water meter must be paid before the permit is issued, where they apply. Not every addition attracts them, but you want to know which apply to yours before you set a budget.
  • Zoning. The addition has to comply with the Zoning By-law: setbacks, coverage and height all limit where the new footprint can go.
  • The conservation authority. The guide lists conservation authority approval among the applicable laws. Barrie is split between the Lake Simcoe and Nottawasaga authorities, so a house near a creek, ravine or the bay may need a second sign-off.
  • A corner lot. On a corner, the plans must respect the City's sight triangle standards, the clear area drivers need to see around the corner. An addition toward the side street is where this bites.

The same guide points owners to APLI, the City's online portal, to apply before any work starts, and notes that construction can begin once the permit is issued. It also carries a reminder that is easy to miss: projects that raise a property's assessed value may affect property taxes, and the Municipal Property Assessment Corporation (MPAC) is where to check.

Is it an addition, or a second unit?

Barrie has also changed what a residential lot can hold. In December 2023 the City brought forward an amendment to Zoning By-law 2009-141 to permit up to four dwelling units on residentially zoned parcels. For a house addition in Barrie, that raises a planning question worth settling before the drawings start: is the new space more room for the household that lives there, or a separate home for someone else?

On a Barrie lot, the answer changes the design more than the size does. A suite needs its own entrance, its own egress and often its own services, and the zoning sets separate limits for additional units. A family room does not. Settle it first and the structure can be built for the use it will actually have, including a use that comes later. We build to whatever your designer and the City approve; our job is to make sure the foundation is not the thing that rules an option out.

Why the seam matters more than the room

The new space is the easy part. Where a Barrie house addition goes wrong, it almost always goes wrong at the joint.

Two foundations, one house

The new footing sits beside one that may be decades old. If the two settle differently, the crack shows up in the drywall at the joint. Footings are what we do every day.

Old walls, new walls

The existing house may be framed, block or poured. Tying a concrete wall into it is engineered case by case, and we build exactly to the stamp.

Warm on both sides

A new room that is colder than the rest of the house is the classic addition complaint. Continuous foam on both faces of the wall answers it in the structure itself.

A family living next door

The house stays occupied. Excavation and forming happen outside the existing wall, and the breakthrough comes late, once the new shell is closed in.

How we tie a new addition into a Barrie home

Whatever age your Barrie house is, its foundation was built to a different set of drawings from the one going in beside it. That is the core of the job, and it is where a concrete contractor earns their place on an addition rather than being an afterthought to it.

We start by looking at the existing structure and its grades, because the new footing has to bear at a depth and level that suit the old one. Then the footing and foundation for the new footprint, then the ICF walls, tied into the house to the connection details your designer or engineer specifies. Through all of it we keep the occupied part of the house protected from the weather.

When the walls are up, finishes go over ICF the way they go over framing, so the new part can match the brick or siding of the original or deliberately stand apart from it. Inside, drywall fastens straight to the form; we explain why that works in can you put drywall directly on ICF.

Home Additions Barrie — FAQs

Does every addition in Barrie need a building permit?

Yes. The City of Barrie defines an addition as construction that increases a building's area, by footprint or by storeys, and states that all additions require a building permit.

My house is on septic. Does that matter?

It does. The City requires the existing septic system to be reviewed by a qualified designer to confirm it complies with the Ontario Building Code before an addition goes ahead.

Will an addition raise my property taxes?

It may. The City notes that projects which increase a property's assessed value may affect property taxes, and points owners to MPAC for details.

Can an addition become a separate unit?

Possibly. Barrie moved to permit up to four dwelling units on residentially zoned parcels by amending Zoning By-law 2009-141. A separate unit has its own zoning limits, so decide the use before the design is drawn.

Can you tie ICF into a framed or block house?

Yes. The connection is engineered for each house, and we build it to the engineer's details.

Do we have to move out while you build?

Usually not. The structural work happens outside the existing wall, and the opening between old and new is left until the new shell is closed in.
