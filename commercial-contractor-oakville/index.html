Every non-residential building in Oakville starts at planning

If you are choosing a commercial contractor Oakville will accept drawings from, the first thing to know is that the building permit is the second approval, not the first. The Town of Oakville's site plan application form, under Site Plan By-law 2025-033, lists full site plan approval as required for all non-residential development. The Town's page on non-residential new construction is just as direct: you must apply for site plan approval before applying for a building permit.

That order has a consequence for how the building is built. By the time site plan is approved, the footprint, the elevations, the grading and the servicing are fixed. Changing the wall system after that is not a substitution; it is a redesign that walks back through review. So the structural decision, insulated concrete or something else, belongs at the start, when the site plan is still a sketch.

On commercial work we deliver the concrete scope, footings, foundations and full-height ICF walls, either under your general contractor or carried through to the above-grade walls.

Oakville commercial construction, in the Town's order

The form describes pre-consultation as strongly encouraged, a high-level review before formal submission that can cut down resubmissions and that scopes the plans, reports and studies the Official Plan requires. For a building that will be concrete from the footing up, that meeting is the place to say so. A geotechnical question raised there is a study you plan for; raised at permit, it is a delay.

Oakville's site plan stage also carries money that is easy to leave out of an early budget. The form sets lump-sum securities for commercial, employment and institutional sites at $50,000 per hectare plus $500 per metre of street frontage, alongside a variable application fee of $1,204 per 100 square metres of non-residential gross floor area. The form adds a recirculation fee of 15 per cent of the application fee for every circulation after the third, which is one more reason to settle the structure before the drawings start going round.

What the building permit asks for, and where the walls show up

Once site plan is in hand, the Town's non-residential new construction page sets out the permit package. It notes that an architect, engineer or qualified BCIN designer is typically retained, and it lists, among other items:

  • Site plan, architectural, structural and mechanical drawings, and the OBC matrix
  • A commitment to have general review carried out by the architects or engineers
  • Geotechnical calculations, or a subsurface investigation declaration
  • The SB-10 form, with its supporting drawings, documents and calculations
  • A controlled flow roof drain declaration
  • The non-residential development charges form, and the development finance sign-off where it applies
  • Fire alarm, sprinkler, plumbing and kitchen suppression drawings where those systems change

On an Oakville permit, two of those are where the structural system is judged. The geotechnical item is what the footings are designed to, and engineered footing packages are the base of everything we build. The SB-10 form is the energy compliance submission, and it is where a wall's continuous insulation gets counted. An ICF wall carries foam on both faces of a reinforced concrete core, for an effective R-22 to R-26, and we install cores of 6, 8, 10 and 12 inches, sized to the engineer's design. Give your energy consultant the actual assembly early and the numbers on that form reflect the building you mean to put up.

Development charges, and an industrial exception

In any commercial construction Oakville budget, development charges are a large line. The Town of Oakville collects them for itself, for Halton Region and for the local school boards. Its development charges page says they apply when you construct a new building, add to or alter one in a way that increases floor area or units, or redevelop with a change of use. On non-residential work the Town's construction page adds the mechanics: a non-residential development charge form goes in with the permit so the Region can review the scope, and charges must be paid before the permit is issued.

One exemption matters to anyone running an industrial building in Oakville. The Town lists the enlargement of an industrial building, up to a maximum of 50 per cent of its gross floor area, among the mandated exemptions. If you are expanding a plant or warehouse rather than building new, that line can change the arithmetic of the whole project. Demolition credits are also provided against charges, based on the floor area demolished, for five years from the demolition permit.

Timing in Oakville is worth watching too. The current charges sit under By-law 2022-068, and the Town says it is undergoing a development charge background study, with a proposed new by-law going to Council in early 2027. The education portion alone is listed at $33.90 per square metre of non-residential floor area as of June 1, 2026. Confirm the rates in force on the day your permit will issue.

The other agencies at the table

A commercial construction company Oakville trusts with a site has to plan for more than the Town. The Town's own pages name who else may be involved, depending on the property and the use.

On an Oakville site, two of these touch the foundation directly. Conservation Halton regulates development in and near watercourses, floodplains, valley lands, wetlands and the Lake Ontario shoreline under Ontario Regulation 41/24, and the Town's site plan form also names Credit Valley Conservation for review fees, so check which authority covers your parcel. And an approved fire route, which the Town requires, fixes where heavy vehicles can reach the building, which is also where a pump truck can set up on pour day.

The others depend on what the Oakville building will be used for. The Town's page lists a record of site condition from the Ministry of the Environment when a property moves to a more sensitive use, such as a daycare in a former industrial building, and Ministry of Transportation approval near designated highways.

Commercial Contractor Oakville — FAQs

Does a commercial building in Oakville need site plan approval?

Yes. Under Site Plan By-law 2025-033 the Town requires full site plan approval for all non-residential development, and site plan comes before the building permit application.

Is pre-consultation required?

The Town's site plan form calls a pre-consultation meeting strongly encouraged. It gives a high-level review before submission and scopes the plans, reports and studies the Official Plan requires.

When are development charges paid on commercial work?

Before the building permit is issued. A non-residential development charge form is submitted with the permit so the Region can review the scope and confirm which charges apply.

Is an industrial expansion exempt from development charges?

Partly. The Town lists the enlargement of an industrial building up to a maximum of 50 per cent of its gross floor area among the mandated exemptions.

Do you work under a general contractor?

Yes. We regularly deliver the concrete and ICF scope for builders and GCs, and can also carry it from the footings through to the above-grade walls.

Are you based in Oakville?

No. We are based in Barrie, and Oakville is one of the Greater Toronto Area towns on our published service area.
