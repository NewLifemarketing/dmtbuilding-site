You can buy the block anywhere. You cannot buy the pour.

Search for insulated concrete forms Aurora and half of what comes back is selling block: foam forms by the pallet, delivered to site. That is a fair thing to want, and it is not what we do. DMT Building Group is an installer and builder. Our crews are certified on Nudura, Amvic, Element and Fox; you pick the system and we build with it, buying through the ICF supply houses we list on our partners page.

The distinction matters more in Aurora than the price per block does. A stack of forms on a pallet has no inspection, no survey and no permit. The moment it becomes a foundation in Aurora, it picks up all three, and the Town's Building By-law 6096-18 asks for more paper on the foundation than on any other stage of a new house. This page walks through that record, in the order Aurora asks for it, and what an installer has to do to meet each part.

Our office is in Barrie, Ontario. Aurora is one of the Greater Toronto Area towns on the service area we publish, and our own crews travel to it.

What Aurora asks for before the first form is set

Three requirements in Section 15 of Aurora's building by-law apply to an Aurora foundation and are settled before anyone digs.

  • A geotechnical report. Section 15.5 says every application to construct a new building, or any other where the Chief Building Official requires it, comes with a geotechnical report by a professional engineer. That report is what the footing is designed to. Footing width, depth and bearing come from it, not from habit.
  • A surveyed site plan with grades. Section 15.4 requires a site plan referenced to a current survey by an Ontario Land Surveyor, showing setbacks and the existing and finished ground levels. The finished grade sets how much ICF stands above ground and where the foundation drainage has to work.
  • Foundation plans. Schedule C of the by-law lists foundation plans as their own drawing set, separate from structural plans and sections. On an ICF job those plans carry the core thickness, the reinforcement and the openings that get formed into the wall.

Section 15.6 adds one more the Town can ask for: an engineer's report on vibration from the construction methods and how it will be controlled. On an Aurora infill lot with neighbours close by, that is worth expecting.

Aurora's Building Division reviews all of this electronically. Its submission guidelines ask for each drawing type as a separate PDF, named for what it is, and the Town lists "foundation" by name among the file types it expects. It also prefers the designer's BCIN stamp built into the PDF rather than scanned.

Six checkpoints between the footing and the framing

Once the permit is issued, Aurora's by-law sets the points at which Aurora's inspectors look at the work. Two are prescribed inspection notices in Schedule E. The third is a survey the Chief Building Official may require under Section 15.7. Laid out in order, they show exactly where a foundation installer's work is checked.

Checkpoint five is the one that tells you whether an installer is any good. The surveyor measures where the foundation wall actually is and how high its top actually sits, against the approved site plan, and the Town can hold the framing inspection until it has that survey. A wall that wandered a few centimetres during the pour, or a top that came in high, is on paper before a single joist goes down. On an Aurora lot where the setbacks or the height are tight, there is very little room for either.

ICF home builders Aurora projects call in for the foundation stage

Most of the calls we take come from builders who already know the product. They bring us in for the below-grade stage and take over a level, square, backfill-ready foundation; homeowners can keep us on for the above-grade walls. Aurora's permit process has a mechanism that suits the first arrangement on larger Aurora projects.

Aurora's Policy for Partial Permits, updated January 2026, lets the Chief Building Official issue a building in stages, at the CBO's discretion. One of the stages is a foundation permit, which the policy says covers all below-grade structural construction, including the ground floor structural system. Its conditions are specific:

Full application first

The application and fees for the whole project go in before any partial permit is requested, with a site plan marked "for foundation only" and complete structural drawings.

Site plan nearly done

The Planner confirms final comments from circulated agencies are in and the Site Plan Agreement fees and securities are paid.

Other approvals in place

Conservation authority, the Ministry of Environment where a Record of Site Condition applies, and the Ministry of Transportation.

Charges paid

All development charges and cash-in-lieu of parkland are paid, and an inside buried drains permit is issued with the foundation permit.

The minimum fee the policy lists for a foundation permit is $687. The practical point for a builder is that the foundation can start while the rest of the approvals finish, but only once the money and the agency sign-offs are in. Our Pebble Ridge project story shows the kind of package that fits this stage: long runs of strip footing, deep reinforced pads and dowelled connections, laid out and poured in sequence and checked against the engineering at each step.

What an installer puts into the wall

The foundation plans Aurora asks for describe a finished wall, not how it gets built. Getting from an Aurora drawing to that wall is the installer's whole job, and most of it is invisible once the backfill is in.

Our sequence is the one Aurora's inspections above check. Footings, pads and piers are formed to the soil, then weeper and interior stone go in so drainage is there before the walls go up. The insulated concrete walls are set, braced and aligned on Giraffe scaffold, poured in controlled lifts and checked as we go, with window and door bucks, beam pockets and service penetrations formed in. Waterproofing and drainage go on the outside face, and the floor assembly lands on the ledger and hanger connections the engineering specifies.

We are also the people who will take a line off the quote. A second drainage board, waterproofing on a frost wall or membrane under the basement slab is usually not needed, and we would rather tell you than sell it. Our post on whether ICF foundations need waterproofing explains why.

Insulated Concrete Forms Aurora — FAQs

Do you sell ICF blocks in Aurora?

No. We are installers and builders. Our crews are certified on Nudura, Amvic, Element and Fox, and we build with whichever of those systems you pick.

Does Aurora require a geotechnical report for a new house?

Aurora's Building By-law 6096-18 says applications to construct a new building are to be accompanied by a geotechnical report prepared by a professional engineer, or where the Chief Building Official requires one.

What inspections does a foundation get in Aurora?

The by-law's required inspection notices include readiness to construct footings, and substantial completion of footings and foundations before backfilling. The Chief Building Official may also require a surveyor's survey of the foundation's location and top-of-wall elevation before the framing inspection.

Can the foundation get its own permit?

On projects going through site plan approval, Aurora's Policy for Partial Permits allows a foundation permit at the Chief Building Official's discretion, once the full application is in, development charges are paid and other approvals are in place. The minimum fee listed is $687.

What core size will my foundation use?

We build with 6", 8", 10" and 12" cores. A typical home lands on a 6" or 8" core; the engineering and the site decide.

Are you based in Aurora?

No. We are based in Barrie, and Aurora is one of the Greater Toronto Area towns on our published service area.
