In Burlington, the paperwork comes first and it is not short

Any commercial construction company Burlington property owners hire will hand them the same calendar: a long stretch of approvals, then a build that everyone wants finished yesterday. That shape is set by the City of Burlington and Halton Region, not by the contractor, and it is worth understanding before you choose one.

Three Burlington rules set it. Site Plan Control By-law 17-2026 designates the whole of the City of Burlington as a site plan control area. A non-residential zoning clearance certificate is required for industrial, commercial and institutional uses. And Halton Region collects its development charges on non-residential buildings before the building permit is issued, not at occupancy as it does for houses.

Each of those lands before a shovel goes into a Burlington site. By the time the permit is in hand, the owner has usually spent months and paid the Region, and the question becomes how quickly the structure can go up.

Site plan control covers the whole city

Section 3 of Burlington's Site Plan Control By-law is one sentence long, and it settles a great deal: the whole City of Burlington is a site plan control area. The exemptions in Section 6 are mostly houses, accessory buildings, farm buildings, EV chargers, mechanical upgrades and exterior work that adds no floor area. A new commercial or industrial building is not on that list, and neither is an addition to one.

Burlington leaves one narrow door. Under Section 8, the Director may exempt a project whose new floor area is the lesser of 5% of the building's total or 30 m², but only if criteria in Section 7 are met, and one of those is that the property does not hold part of the Natural Heritage System or a Conservation Halton regulated area. For most Burlington commercial construction, plan on site plan approval.

The City of Burlington's site plans page describes the steps plainly. You request a pre-consultation online, with a fee. The request is circulated with a four-week comment period, and the City says it takes four to six weeks from submission to the meeting. Then the full application is deemed complete or put on hold, circulated, and returned with a Summary of Comments until every department and agency is satisfied. Staff issue a Conditional Approval, you enter a Site Plan Control Agreement, and that agreement must be registered on title before Final Approval and before your building permit application can be submitted.

Burlington commercial construction still runs on By-law 2020

Burlington replaced its residential zoning on March 2, 2026. Commercial and employment land did not move with it. The City's zoning page lists two by-laws in force, and Zoning By-law 2020 still governs the commercial zones (CR, CC1, CC2, CE, CN1, CN2), the employment zones (BC1 and BC2 Business Corridor, GE1 and GE2 General Employment), the mixed-use corridors and the downtown and uptown centres.

The General Employment rules in Part 3 of Burlington's 2020 by-law are the ones that most change the shape of a building, and they are at their strictest where industry meets houses:

  • Yards on named roads. 15 m on Guelph Line and King Road, 6 m on Mainway and Enfield Road, and 9 m on Heritage Road in GE1.
  • Decorative finish. Elevations facing a street or a residential zone must keep a decorative exterior finish.
  • Office-led buildings. Where the principal use is office, the floor area ratio is capped at 0.5:1.
  • Outdoor storage. Not in the front yard on lots under 4 ha, and never within 9 m of a residential zone.

Two of those touch the structure directly. A wall that must carry a decorative finish needs a face that will take one; siding, stone, brick and stucco all go over an ICF wall the way they go over framing. And on a lot held to 9 m beside houses, the footprint and the foundation under it do more of the work of fitting the program in.

Halton's charges are due before the permit

For a house in Burlington, Halton Region collects its development charges before first occupancy. For non-residential development, its schedule is different: general services, water, wastewater and roads charges are all collected before the building permit is issued, at the rate in force when the permit is issued. The Region indexes those rates every April 1, and the City of Burlington's own development charge rates took effect on June 1, 2026.

The Region's page is also clear that charges can apply to more than a new building. Adding to an existing building in a way that increases its total floor area can trigger them. So can a tenant fit-out in a shell building that changes the use the original charges were paid for. If you are planning an expansion or a re-tenanting rather than a new build, ask the Region and the City before you budget.

The practical effect for a Burlington owner is cash out before construction starts. Every week between paying the Region and closing in the building is a week of carrying cost with nothing to show for it, which is where the structure and how fast it goes up start to matter.

Commercial Construction Company Burlington — FAQs

Does my Burlington commercial project need site plan approval?

Almost certainly. Site Plan Control By-law 17-2026 makes the whole city a site plan control area. Exemptions cover houses, farm buildings, mechanical upgrades and exterior alterations that add no floor area, not new commercial or industrial buildings.

When can I apply for the building permit?

After Final Site Plan Approval. The City of Burlington requires the Site Plan Control Agreement to be registered on title before final approval is issued and before a building permit application may be submitted.

When are Halton's development charges due on a commercial building?

For non-residential development, Halton Region collects its charges before the building permit is issued, at the rate in force when the permit is issued.

Which zoning by-law covers employment land in Burlington?

Zoning By-law 2020. The new Residential Zoning By-law of March 2, 2026 covers residential areas only; the commercial, employment and mixed-use zones remain under By-law 2020.

Can you work under our general contractor?

Yes. We regularly deliver the concrete and ICF scope for builders and general contractors, from footings through to the walls.

Do you have an office in Burlington?

No. We are based in Barrie and build south into the Greater Toronto Area.
