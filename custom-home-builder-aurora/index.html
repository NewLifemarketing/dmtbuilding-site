Aurora is built on a watershed divide

The Lake Simcoe Region Conservation Authority describes the Oak Ridges Moraine as the southern edge of its watershed: land drains north off the moraine to Lake Simcoe, and south off it to Lake Ontario. Aurora sits across that line. Its own zoning by-law says so in plain terms, naming both the Lake Simcoe Region Conservation Authority and the Toronto and Region Conservation Authority as the agencies whose floodplain, erosion and steep-slope mapping make up the Town's Natural Hazards overlay.

That is the first thing a custom home builder Aurora owners talk to should know about a lot here: which side of the divide it is on, and what the moraine itself allows. On most streets the answer is simple. On the lots people buy for their trees, their slope and their quiet, it can decide the size of the house before anyone draws it.

We are DMT Building Group, based in Barrie, Ontario. Aurora is one of the Greater Toronto Area towns on the service area we publish, and we build custom homes there in insulated concrete form (ICF), with our own crews doing the footings, foundations and walls. This page walks through the maps Aurora publishes and what each one means for a new home in Aurora.

Six maps under every Aurora zone

Aurora's Comprehensive Zoning By-law 6000-17 was approved by Council on June 27, 2017, the first full review since 1979. Look a property up and you get its zone. Underneath the zone are schedules that can add rules, and on the moraine they can take over from the zone entirely.

Section 6 of the by-law states that the Natural Hazards overlay takes precedence over the underlying zone, and that LSRCA and TRCA regulations may restrict development and may be a precondition to it. Wellhead Protection Areas come from the Clean Water Act, 2006, carried through York Region's and Aurora's official plans, and the Region may require a Risk Management Plan before development. Those two layers can touch any lot in Aurora. The other three only matter on the moraine, and there they matter a lot.

Custom home builders Aurora: what the moraine allows

Section 14 of Aurora's by-law applies to everything inside the Oak Ridges Moraine Conservation Plan Area, and its reference date is November 15, 2001. Uses that legally existed on that date can continue. Anything new is measured against the moraine schedules.

Three rules do most of the work for a new house:

  • Features are off the table. On the part of a lot inside a key natural heritage feature, a hydrologically sensitive feature, or the minimum vegetation protection zone around one, as shown on Schedule B, no development or site alteration may happen without a zoning amendment or relief from the by-law.
  • Settlement-area lots with an ORM suffix have their own siting. Where the zone symbol is followed by "ORM", the by-law sets a 15 metre front yard, 22 metre rear yard, interior side yards of 9.0 and 4.5 metres, a 15 metre exterior side yard and a 10 metre height limit.
  • Landform caps the footprint. In a Category 1 Landform Conservation Area, the disturbed part of the site may not exceed 25 per cent of the total site area, and impervious surfaces may not exceed 15 per cent. In Category 2 the limits are 50 and 20 per cent.

Read that chart as a builder would. The disturbed area is not just the house. It is the excavation, the driveway, the grading and the working room around the foundation. On a Category 1 lot, a quarter of the site has to hold all of it, and the hard surfaces have to fit in less than a sixth. Section 14.4.3 adds that development on Category 1 and 2 lands needs a zoning amendment or relief under Official Plan Amendment 48, so on those lots the drawings go to Planning before they go anywhere near a permit counter.

What that means for how the house is built

When the ground you may touch is capped, the foundation stops being a line item and becomes the plan. A footprint that stacks its floor area up rather than spreading it out disturbs less of the lot. A foundation that steps with the existing slope moves less earth than one that levels it. Both are design choices your architect and engineer make, and both are things our crews build.

One practical note for moraine lots inside a Wellhead Protection Area: Section 14.3 lists storage of construction equipment among the uses prohibited there, except by an individual for personal or family use. Where your lot sits in one, ask the Town how it applies to site staging before the excavator arrives.

Aurora's older streets

In Aurora's established neighbourhoods, the questions change from ground to neighbourhood. Aurora has protected Northeast Old Aurora as a Heritage Conservation District under Part V of the Ontario Heritage Act. Inside the district, the Town reviews heritage permits for alterations and new construction against the District Plan, and the whole property except the interior is designated.

For established low-rise areas outside the district, the Town has published area-specific Stable Neighbourhood Urban Design Guidelines for infill in Regency Acres, Temperance Street, Town Park and Aurora Heights. If your lot is in one of those four, read its guideline before you brief a designer.

Trees are the third question. Aurora's Private Tree Protection By-law 5850-16 lets an owner remove two trees in a 12-month period without a permit, or two per quarter hectare on a larger property. A single heritage tree, or a single tree in any heritage district, needs a permit before it comes down.

Permits and charges for a new home in Aurora

Aurora's Building Division reviews permit applications electronically. They can go in through the Town's online portal, by email, or on a USB drive delivered in person, all as unlocked PDFs in black and white, with each drawing type saved as a separate file. A new building may also need a separate plumbing permit. The Town prefers the designer's BCIN stamp and signature built into the electronic drawings rather than scanned.

Development charges are the number most people underestimate. For a single or semi-detached house, the rates Aurora publishes for July 1 to November 7, 2026 are $41,301 for the Town, $84,718 for York Region and $8,934 for education, a total of $134,953 per unit. The Town says final charges are calculated only when it reviews the final construction drawings for the building permit.

One more thing that does not usually apply to a single custom house: Aurora's Green Development Standards are applied to plans of subdivision with at least five units and to major site plan applications. If you are building one home on one lot, the energy standard you meet is the Ontario Building Code's, which is where the performance of an ICF wall against a conventional one shows up on paper.

Home Builders Aurora — FAQs

How do I know if my Aurora lot is on the Oak Ridges Moraine?

Aurora's zoning by-law shows the Oak Ridges Moraine Conservation Plan Area on Schedule A, and the Town publishes an interactive zoning web application. Where the zone symbol ends in "ORM", the moraine siting rules in Section 14 apply.

Which conservation authority covers Aurora?

Both LSRCA and TRCA. Aurora's Natural Hazards overlay is built from their floodplain, erosion and steep-slope mapping, and either authority's regulations may restrict development or be a precondition to it.

How much of a moraine lot can I build on?

In a Category 1 Landform Conservation Area, no more than 25 per cent of the site may be disturbed and no more than 15 per cent may be impervious. In Category 2 the limits are 50 and 20 per cent. Development on those lands also needs a zoning amendment or relief.

What are the development charges on a new house in Aurora?

For a single or semi-detached house, Aurora publishes a total of $134,953 per unit for July 1 to November 7, 2026: $41,301 Town, $84,718 York Region and $8,934 education. Final charges are calculated from the final permit drawings.

Can I remove trees before building?

Aurora's by-law 5850-16 allows two trees in 12 months without a permit, or two per quarter hectare on larger properties. A heritage tree, or any tree in a heritage district, needs a permit.

Are you based in Aurora?

No. We are based in Barrie, and Aurora is one of the GTA towns on our published service area.
