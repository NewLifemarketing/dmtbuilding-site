In Mississauga, the teardown and the new house are one permit story

Anyone talking to custom home builders Mississauga homeowners recommend will hear about design first. The City of Mississauga starts somewhere else. Its Demolition Control By-law 45-19 designates the entire city as a demolition control area, and no one may demolish a residential property in it without a demolition control permit. The by-law exists, in Council's own words, to prevent the premature loss of housing stock and the creation of vacant land.

For a replacement house, the by-law sets the order of operations. A demolition control permit is issued where a building permit has already been issued for the replacement building on the same site. So the new house gets its permit first, and only then does the old one come down. For a property with fewer than six units, the Chief Building Official issues the demolition permit, with the owner's consent to any conditions attached to it.

The conditions that can come with a Mississauga demolition permit

Section 11 of the Mississauga by-law lists the conditions the City may impose. They are not guaranteed to appear on every permit, but every owner of custom homes Mississauga is about to tear down should read them before signing anything:

  • A completion date. The replacement building must be built and substantially completed by a date set in the permit, no sooner than two years from the day demolition of the existing house begins.
  • A financial consequence. If the replacement is not finished in time, the City may add the sum specified in the permit, up to $20,000 for each demolished dwelling unit, to the tax roll, where it forms a lien on the land.
  • Notice on title. The applicant may be required to register both conditions on title to the property.

Two years is a reasonable runway for a single house. It gets shorter in practice when the excavation hits something unexpected, when a foundation waits for good weather, or when the structure is waiting on three trades who each wait on the one before. That is the part of a Mississauga schedule where who builds the structure, and how, actually matters.

Why the structure is where custom home builders in Mississauga win or lose time

An ICF wall puts the concrete structure, the continuous insulation and the surface the finishes fasten to into one wall cycle. Our crews self-perform the footings, the foundation, the walls and the waterproofing, so there is no hand-off between a foundation sub, a framer and an insulator on the part of the house the demolition clock is really measuring.

We also keep building through the winter. Our crews detail and pour ICF walls in February, under insulated tarps when the weather calls for it, which on a Mississauga replacement house means a demolition in the fall does not have to wait for spring before the foundation goes in. If you want to see how that is done, our post on pouring concrete in winter covers it.

And infill lots are tight. On our Teardown & Rebuild site the neighbours were a few metres away on both sides, machine access was limited, and the spoil had to leave the site. We sequenced the excavation and forming so the hole stayed open for as little time as possible. On a Mississauga street of existing houses, that is most of the job.

What the new house can be: no site plan, but tight infill limits

The good news for anyone choosing between home builders in Mississauga is that a single replacement house does not need site plan approval. The City's Site Plan Control By-law 0293-2006 exempts residential development of 10 units or less. There is no Mississauga design panel to win over.

The zoning still has the last word, and in many of Mississauga's established neighbourhoods it says more than the base rules. The same table caps gross floor area at 190 m² plus 0.20 times the lot area, limits dwelling unit depth to 20.0 m, and allows a garage to project 0.0 m beyond the front wall of the first storey. A new house there is designed inside a box the zoning has already drawn. That is one more reason to settle the design, and the structure that carries it, before demolition starts rather than after.

Before the excavator: what a Mississauga replacement house needs lined up

Each of these is on the City's own checklists or the relevant authority's pages. Each one is cheaper to settle while the old Mississauga house is still standing.

The new house permit

Through ePlans: a site plan with site statistics, a grading plan, floor plans with foundation specifications, the Applicable Law Form, the SB-12 energy summary and the Single Dwelling Unit Information Letter.

Trees on the lot

A Mississauga permit is needed to injure or remove any private tree 15 cm or more in diameter, and the City requires arborist reports for construction projects. One replacement tree per 15 cm of trunk removed.

The creek and the lake

Credit Valley Conservation regulates most of Mississauga, TRCA the Etobicoke and Mimico creek watersheds. Near a valley, floodplain, wetland or the Lake Ontario shore, either may require its own permit.

Charges and credits

Development charges are paid before the permit is issued. The City notes a demolition credit may apply where residential units are demolished and replaced, subject to time limits in the by-laws.

One more check before you buy or design: Mississauga has three heritage conservation districts, Meadowvale Village, Old Port Credit Village and Streetsville, and more than a thousand listed heritage properties. Demolition in or of one of those is a different conversation.

Custom Home Builders in Mississauga — FAQs

Can I demolish my house in Mississauga before I have a permit for the new one?

Under Demolition Control By-law 45-19, the whole city is a demolition control area. A demolition control permit is issued where a building permit has been issued for the replacement building, so the new house is normally permitted first.

Is there a deadline to finish the new house?

There can be. The City may require the replacement to be substantially complete by a date in the permit, no sooner than two years after demolition begins, and may add up to $20,000 per demolished unit to the tax roll if it is late.

Does a new single house need site plan approval in Mississauga?

Generally no. Site Plan Control By-law 0293-2006 exempts residential development of 10 units or less. The house must still meet the Zoning By-law.

How tall can a new house be?

The base maximum in R1 to R5 is 10.7 m. Where the R1 infill exception applies, a sloped roof ridge is capped at 9.5 m or 9.0 m depending on frontage, a flat roof at 7.5 m and eaves at 6.4 m.

Do development charges apply to a replacement house?

Charges are paid before the building permit is issued. The City notes a demolition credit may apply where residential units are demolished and replaced, subject to the expiry dates in the by-laws.

Are you based in Mississauga?

No. We are based in Barrie and build across the Greater Toronto Area, including Mississauga.
