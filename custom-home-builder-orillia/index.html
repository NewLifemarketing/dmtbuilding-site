One number decides a low Orillia lot: 221.2 metres

Before you hire a custom home builder Orillia homeowners would recommend, find out whether your lot sits in the City's Flood Hazard Overlay Zone. If it does, one figure will shape the house more than any floor plan: 221.2 metres above sea level, measured on the Canadian Geodetic Survey datum.

That is the Flood Construction Level in the City of Orillia's policy POL.BD.OBC.29, Building in Flood Plains. The Chief Building Official issued it in 2024 and revised it in May 2026. It adds a 0.3 metre freeboard to a Regulatory Flood Elevation of 220.9 metres, and it applies to new buildings, additions, alterations to building systems and septic systems on any land the Zoning By-law marks as subject to flooding.

The rule for a new house is short. Every square foot of habitable floor, and every opening in the building, has to sit at or above 221.2 metres. The structure has to be designed to withstand flood forces. And the design has to be prepared, signed and sealed by a Professional Engineer licensed in Ontario.

We are DMT Building Group, based in Barrie, Ontario. Orillia is one of the Simcoe County towns on our published service area, and we build custom homes there in insulated concrete form (ICF), with our own crews on the footings, the foundation, the walls and the waterproofing. On a lot below the flood line, that part of the house is the part the City is asking about.

What the Orillia flood policy looks like through a wall

Read the Orillia policy as a section drawing and the job becomes clear. Everything people live in goes above the red line. Everything below it is structure, and that structure has to stand up to standing water pushing on it and moving water pushing past it. The policy names both forces, hydrostatic and hydrodynamic, and asks for "good engineering practice" against them.

That is a description of a foundation wall. On an Orillia lot in the overlay, the foundation is not just what the house sits on. It is the floodproofing.

What a complete application in the overlay has to show

The Orillia policy is specific about paperwork. For a new building in Orillia's Flood Hazard Overlay Zone, a complete permit application includes:

  • Engineered drawings and specifications
  • A floodproofing design report
  • Confirmation of geodetic elevations, with a site plan and a grading plan showing them
  • Geotechnical certification, if fill is being used to raise the house to the required elevation
  • Electrical, mechanical, plumbing and heating located above the Flood Construction Level, or protected and accessible where they cannot be
  • Backflow prevention where it is needed against surcharge in the sanitary and storm systems
  • For a septic system, the bottom of the gravel layer at or above 221.2 metres, and tanks below the flood elevation anchored against flotation

Two more items arrive later and catch people out. The City of Orillia may register warning clauses or maintenance agreements on title for the floodproofing. And before an occupancy permit is issued, an Ontario Land Surveyor or a Professional Engineer has to certify that the building's elevations match the approved plans and that the Flood Construction Level has been met.

That last certificate is measured off what we build. The top of the foundation sets the floor, and the floor sets every opening above it. If the walls come out a few centimetres low, the surveyor finds it at the end, when the only fix left is an expensive one. We brace and align every wall with Giraffe bracing and alignment scaffold before concrete goes in, and we work to the engineer's stamped drawings, flagging anything on site that does not match them.

Orillia's flood maps are being redrawn

Orillia's 221.2 metre figure is applied on an interim basis. The policy says so directly: the Chief Building Official applies it notwithstanding any different elevation in the Zoning By-law, based on the most current engineering study, until the by-law is updated.

That update is under way. Orillia has said it is replacing floodplain mapping that is 35 years old and folding the revised maps and policies into a new Official Plan, with Council adoption targeted for fall 2026. For the Ben's Ditch Flood Hazard Area, the City has proposed a Two-Zone approach, which separates a "floodway" from a "flood fringe" instead of treating the whole hazard area as one zone. A public information centre on that proposal was held on July 16, 2026.

For anyone buying a lot in Orillia to build on, the practical consequence is simple. Check the lot against the current Zoning By-law 2014-44, whose Schedule B is the overlay zones map, and ask the City whether the new mapping changes anything for that property. A lot that sits just outside today's line, or just inside it, is worth a phone call before an offer.

Orillia also sits inside a larger system. The Lake Simcoe Region Conservation Authority describes the Lake Simcoe watershed as running through York and Durham regions, Simcoe County and the cities of Kawartha Lakes, Barrie and Orillia. Which agencies review a particular Orillia lot is a question for the City of Orillia at the start, and we will ask it with you.

Why we build Orillia homes in insulated concrete

Plenty of new home builders Orillia families talk to will frame a house well. The question on a low lot is narrower: who builds the part below the flood line? Where the City wants the structure engineered against flood forces and the floor certified to a geodetic elevation, splitting the foundation and the walls between two contractors splits the most important part of the house.

An ICF wall puts reinforced concrete inside continuous foam, from the footing to the roof plate if you want it to go that far. The same crew forms the footings, sets and braces the block, places the steel, pours the walls and applies the waterproofing. One accountable firm owns everything below the flood line, which is the part of an Orillia house the floodproofing report is written about.

The rest of the Orillia new house permit

Flood or no flood, every new dwelling in Orillia goes through the same front door. The City's own permit guide for detached, semi-detached and townhouse dwellings sets the order.

A Zoning Certificate comes first, before the residential building permit application. The application then needs the City's forms (not needed when you apply through the public portal), floor plans, elevations, cross sections and details, heat loss and heat gain where they apply, engineered truss and floor layouts where they apply, and a lot grading plan designed and stamped by a professional engineer. It goes in through the online public portal or by email to the building division.

The guide lists the typical charges too: building permit fees, City development charges, education development charges for both the Simcoe County District School Board and the Simcoe Muskoka Catholic District School Board, and a $2,000 lot grading deposit, of which $1,650 comes back once the grading is certified. The current Orillia rates are set by the City and the boards; ask for them against your specific lot.

The energy side matters more for an ICF house than for most. The Energy Efficiency Design Summary and the heat loss calculations are where a continuous insulated concrete wall gets credit on paper, so have them done by someone who knows how to model one.

Custom Home Builder Orillia — FAQs

How do I know if my Orillia lot is in the flood hazard area?

The Flood Hazard Overlay Zone is identified in Zoning By-law 2014-44; Schedule B is the overlay zones map. The City is replacing its 35-year-old floodplain mapping through a new Official Plan, so confirm the lot's status with the City directly.

What is the Flood Construction Level in Orillia?

221.2 metres CGS: a Regulatory Flood Elevation of 220.9 metres plus 0.3 metres of freeboard. The Chief Building Official applies it on an interim basis under policy POL.BD.OBC.29.

Can a new house in the overlay have living space below that level?

No. For a new building, the policy requires all habitable floor space and all building openings to be at or above the Flood Construction Level, with the structure engineered to resist flood forces.

What fees come with a new house permit in Orillia?

The City's guide lists building permit fees, City development charges, education development charges for both school boards, and a $2,000 lot grading deposit, $1,650 of it refundable once grading is certified.

Can you build to my engineer's floodproofing design?

Yes. We build to the engineer's stamp and flag anything on site that does not match the drawings.

Are you based in Orillia?

No. We are based in Barrie, and Orillia is one of the Simcoe County towns on our published service area.
