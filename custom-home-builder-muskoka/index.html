A permanent home is a different project from a cottage

Not everyone looking for a custom home builder Muskoka has to offer is planning a cottage. Some are planning a full-time house: a retirement home on the lake, a family moving north for good, a cottage lot finally getting the house it was bought for. The difference from a seasonal place is not the floor plan. It is that the building has to work every day of the year, on the services and the budget of a permanent home.

That changes three things before any design work: which government you deal with for what, whether the lot is served by a town system or by its own well and septic, and what the ground underneath will take. This page goes through each, with the sources, and then how we build a home here.

We are DMT Building Group, based in Barrie, Ontario, building within about two hours of our office, which takes in all of Muskoka. We build homes in insulated concrete form (ICF) from the footings to the roofline, with our own crews doing the concrete.

Two governments, one house

Muskoka is a two-tier municipality. The District Municipality of Muskoka sits over six area municipalities: the towns of Bracebridge, Gravenhurst and Huntsville, and the townships of Georgian Bay, Lake of Bays and Muskoka Lakes. For a new home, both tiers are involved, and they do different jobs.

Your building permit comes from the area municipality. In Muskoka Lakes, for instance, applications go in online through Cloudpermit. Development charges come from both. The District's current Development Charges By-law, 2024-34, came into effect on January 1, 2025 and runs to the end of 2029, and its own pamphlet tells applicants to consult the area municipality where the building permit is being sought. The township or town then levies its own charge on top; Muskoka Lakes publishes $18,367 for a single-family home for 2026 under its By-law 2024-055.

Two details in those documents are worth knowing when you budget. The District's by-law sets separate charges for urban water and wastewater services and for rural septage, so a lot in town and a lot out on the rock do not attract the same District charges. And in Muskoka Lakes, the township charge for a non-rental home is calculated when a complete permit application is received and is due at or before occupancy. Ask the area municipality for your figures; we put the question on the list at the first site walk so the answer arrives in time to matter.

The ground is the budget

Ask home builders in Muskoka what moves a price most and the honest answer is usually what is under the topsoil. The Township of Muskoka Lakes describes its own shorelines as thin soils over the granite bedrock of the Precambrian shield, and anyone who has watched an excavator stop dead a foot down knows what that means in practice.

A house on that kind of ground does not get a basement dug into it so much as built onto it. Where the rock is exposed, the footings are scribed and pinned to it. Where it slopes, the foundation steps with it. Where there is soil over rock at an uneven depth, the footing design follows whatever the excavation finds. We have written up how footings on bedrock are done in full, and it is the work we get called onto other builders' sites to do.

Water matters as much as rock. Ontario's own page on the Muskoka River watershed counts more than 2,000 lakes in it and names increased flooding among the pressures the watershed faces. For a permanent home near water, that is a reason to think hard about floor elevations, grading and drainage around the foundation, and to choose a wall that will not be harmed by getting wet. A concrete wall inside foam has no framing in it to rot.

What custom home builders in Muskoka should be building for

A permanent home asks more of the structure than a seasonal one. These are the things we build for on every Muskoka house.

Every day of winter

Continuous foam on both faces of the concrete, with no studs breaking it, is what keeps a lakeside house comfortable when the wind comes off the ice.

A lower level you live in

A walkout on a sloped lot is half the reason to build there. An ICF lower level is warm and drywall-ready from the day it is poured.

Views formed in

Window bucks are set in the forms before the pour, so the openings that face the lake are part of the structure from the first course.

Nothing to rot

Concrete and foam give moisture, pests and freeze-thaw little to work on, which matters on a house near water that you plan to keep.

The lakeside home we point people to

Our Lakeside ICF Build is the clearest picture of the kind of permanent home that suits Muskoka ground. The lot dropped toward the water with little level ground to work from. The foundation stepped with the grade, the walkout level opened to the lake, and window bucks were placed to frame the water from the walkout up. The whole structure went up in ICF, foundation through gable-end walls, for a four-season home with continuous insulation on every level.

The challenge on that job was staging forms, bracing and concrete on a tight, sloped waterfront site. That is the everyday challenge of building a house in Muskoka, and it is why we plan pump-truck positions and access at the site walk rather than on pour day. If you want to see a day like that from the inside, we have written up what happens on ICF pour day.

Custom Home Builder Muskoka — FAQs

Who issues the building permit for a new home in Muskoka?

The area municipality: one of Bracebridge, Gravenhurst, Huntsville, Georgian Bay, Lake of Bays or Muskoka Lakes. The District of Muskoka levies its own development charges alongside.

How much are development charges on a new house?

It depends on the municipality and the lot. Muskoka Lakes publishes $18,367 for a single-family home in 2026, and the District's By-law 2024-34 adds separate charges that differ for lots on urban water and wastewater versus rural septage. Ask the area municipality for your figure.

When are development charges paid in Muskoka Lakes?

For a non-rental home, the township charge is calculated when a complete building permit application is received and is due at or before occupancy.

Can you build on bare rock?

Yes. Footings on bedrock are our specialty: forms scribed to the rock, dowels drilled and epoxy-set into sound stone, and each step poured level.

Are you a Muskoka company?

No. We are based in Barrie and build throughout Muskoka, within about two hours of our office.

Can you work from my architect's plans?

Yes. Most plans convert to ICF cleanly; we review them, flag anything that needs an engineer, and coordinate the changes before construction.
