The moment it adds floor area, it is a different project

A home addition Collingwood homeowners plan usually starts as a sketch of a room. The Town reads it differently. Collingwood treats a renovation inside the walls and an addition that adds floor area as two separate kinds of project, and the line between them is drawn in its own Zoning Certificate rules.

Those rules say a Zoning Certificate is not needed for residential work that does not change the use, increase the gross floor area or create Additional Residential Units. Turn that around and it describes an addition exactly. Once your project adds floor area, Planning Services wants a Zoning Certificate before Building Services will take a permit application, a requirement the Town has had in place since January 1, 2022.

From there, the size of the addition and the address of the house decide what else comes with it. That is what this page walks through, in the order a Collingwood addition meets it.

We are DMT Building Group. We are based in Barrie, Ontario, and Collingwood is on the list of Simcoe County towns we publish as our service area. We build additions in insulated concrete form (ICF) and take on the excavation, footings, foundation and walls ourselves, with one crew accountable for the concrete.

What grows with the size of a Collingwood addition

Collingwood's Residential Additions & Renovations page lists what goes in with the building permit application. Some of it is the same for every addition, whatever its size. One item switches on at a threshold.

For every addition, the Town lists a site plan drawn on the property survey, a foundation plan, a floor plan for each floor including framing, at least three building elevations, and at least one building section. Where they apply, it adds a private sewage system evaluation for a house on septic, and heat loss and heat gain calculations with the furnace make and model and a duct layout.

The threshold is grading. Where the addition is greater than 400 square feet, Collingwood requires a grading plan stamped by a competent person, which it defines as a P.Eng., a C.E.T. or an OLS. Below that size, it is not on the list. That is worth knowing at the sketch stage. An addition drawn at 420 square feet carries a professional grading plan that one drawn at 390 does not, and the plan fixes the ground levels the new foundation is built to.

The Zoning Certificate has its own short list. It needs a scaled site plan showing every existing and proposed building, its height, its setbacks to each lot line, and calculations for lot coverage and landscaped open space. On a tight Collingwood lot, lot coverage is often what decides whether an addition goes out or up.

What the address adds

The second set of questions has nothing to do with size. It is about where the house sits, and Collingwood's own Zoning Certificate page lists them in one place.

  • A heritage address. If the property is in the Downtown Collingwood Heritage Conservation District, or the building is designated under the Ontario Heritage Act, you may need a Heritage Permit.
  • A regulated lot. If the property is in an area regulated by a conservation authority, a separate permit may be required. In Collingwood that can be the Nottawasaga Valley Conservation Authority or the Grey Sauble Conservation Authority, depending on where the lot is.
  • A highway corridor. Within the Ministry of Transportation's controlled area, a separate MTO permit may be required.

The heritage question deserves more than a line. The Town's Heritage Permit Application says that owners in the Heritage Conservation District, or with a designation on a property outside it, must apply for a Heritage Permit before making any exterior alterations, down to painting and window replacements. An addition changes the outside of the house more than either. In the District, the application form asks owners to review the design and material policies of the District Plan first; outside it, the individual designation by-law sets the terms.

Heritage review is not there to stop additions. It does mean the outside of a Collingwood heritage addition is decided by more people than the owner and the designer, and that review runs before the building permit rather than alongside it. For an ICF addition, the useful news is that the structure does not dictate the finish. Exterior cladding goes over an ICF wall the way it goes over framing, so brick, siding, stone or stucco can be chosen to satisfy the District Plan without changing what is underneath.

The Town's interactive Planning Services GIS map shows zoning and heritage information for each property, which makes it the quickest first check on any Collingwood address.

One drawing every Collingwood addition needs

Of everything on the Town's list, the foundation plan is the one that is ours. It is required whether the addition is a 150 square foot mudroom or a two-storey wing, and it is where an ICF addition is decided: footing size and depth, wall thickness, where the new wall meets the old house, and how the new floor lines up with the existing one.

Two lines in the Town's 2026 fee schedule bear on that drawing. Fees for a required permit are due and payable when the application is submitted, not when it is issued, so a complete foundation plan the first time saves paying for revisions. And the schedule lists a staged permit, for a foundation or structural shell, at $340 per application. Whether staging suits a particular addition is the Chief Building Official's call, but it is a route worth asking about when the foundation is ready before the rest of the drawings.

The same schedule notes that an unfinished basement is not counted in a house's floor area for permit fees, and that a future basement finish needs its own permit. If your addition has a full basement under it, finishing that space later is a separate application.

Home Addition Collingwood — FAQs

Do I need a Zoning Certificate for an addition in Collingwood?

Yes, if it adds floor area. The Town only exempts residential work that does not change the use, increase the gross floor area or create Additional Residential Units, and since January 1, 2022 a Zoning Certificate is required before a building permit application.

When does an addition need a stamped grading plan?

Collingwood requires a grading plan stamped by a P.Eng., C.E.T. or OLS where the addition is greater than 400 square feet.

What drawings does a Collingwood addition permit need?

A site plan on the property survey, a foundation plan, floor plans with framing, at least three elevations and at least one section, plus a septic evaluation and heat loss calculations where they apply.

My house is in the heritage district. Can I still add on?

You will likely need a Heritage Permit first. The Town requires one for exterior alterations to properties in the Downtown Collingwood Heritage Conservation District or designated under the Heritage Act, and asks applicants to review the District Plan's design and material policies.

When are permit fees paid in Collingwood?

The Town's 2026 fee schedule says fees for a required permit are due and payable on submission of the application.

Are you based in Collingwood?

No. We are based in Barrie, and Collingwood is one of the Simcoe County towns on our published service area.
