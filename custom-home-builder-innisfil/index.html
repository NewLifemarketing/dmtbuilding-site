Innisfil runs two rulebooks, and your lot is in one of them

If you are talking to a custom home builder Innisfil lot owners have recommended, ask a question most of them will not raise: is the property in Alcona, or does it touch Lake Simcoe? The answer changes which rules the house is drawn to.

For those two areas, the Town of Innisfil does not use an ordinary zoning by-law. It uses Community Planning Permit By-law 058-25, which the Town's own preamble says applies to all properties within the Alcona Primary Settlement Area and all properties along the Lake Simcoe shoreline. Innisfil's scope reaches past the shore itself, to all land under water to the middle of the lake.

Everywhere else in Innisfil, a new house is checked against the zoning by-law and then goes to a building permit, the way it would in most Ontario towns. Inside Innisfil's planning permit area, a step comes before that, and it does a lot of work.

We are DMT Building Group. We are based in Barrie, Ontario, and Innisfil is one of the Simcoe County towns on our published service area. We build custom homes in insulated concrete form (ICF), from the footings to the roofline, with our own crews doing the concrete.

One permit where there used to be three

The Town describes the Community Planning Permit as doing the job of several separate processes at once: minor variance, zoning amendment and site plan control. Instead of a zoning check, then a trip to the committee of adjustment for a variance, then a site plan agreement, an Innisfil shoreline or Alcona lot gets a single Innisfil planning permit that covers all of it.

Innisfil's by-law sets out the order, and it is firm on one point. Section 1.6.6 says that once the planning permit is issued and its conditions are met, the owner may apply for a building permit. Section 1.2.4 is blunter: Innisfil's Chief Building Official may not issue a building permit in the area unless it follows the by-law.

The Innisfil planning permit has its own clock. The by-law says the Town confirms within five business days whether an application is complete and which approval stream it will follow, and decides a complete application within 45 days. Before any of that, it expects a pre-consultation meeting with Town staff and agencies such as the Lake Simcoe Region Conservation Authority, unless the Town confirms one is not needed. For a house in Innisfil's Shoreline Residential precinct, it also asks for summer site photos taken from Lake Simcoe.

Three classes, and why Class 1 is a design brief

Innisfil's By-law 058-25 sorts every application into one of three classes. Which one your house lands in is decided by the drawings, which means it is decided by you and your designer.

Class 1

The application meets every development standard and needs no variation. Town staff approve it, and the by-law requires no public notice.

Class 2

Minor relief from a standard, within the variation limits the by-law tables set. Still a staff decision, and still no notice.

Class 3

Relief beyond those limits, or height above the Class 2 threshold. Council or a delegated committee decides.

What Class 3 adds

Notice to every owner within 120 metres, a sign on the street frontage and on the lake side, and a posting on the Town's website.

The practical reading is simple. If the house fits the precinct's standards, the planning step is a staff review with no neighbours notified. Every metre you push past an Innisfil standard moves the application up a class, adds criteria the Town must be satisfied on, and in Class 3 puts the design in front of Council and the people next door. Some lots justify that. Most Innisfil custom homes do not need to go there if the numbers are known before the first sketch.

The shoreline numbers that draw the house

Innisfil's Shoreline Residential precinct has its own tables, and they are worth reading before you fall in love with a floor plan. For a lot inside a settlement area, Table 5.7 of the by-law sets a waterfront yard of 15 metres where the lot is 60 metres deep or less, and 30 metres from the water's edge where it is deeper. Attached decks, porches and similar structures keep 15 metres back. Maximum lot coverage is 35 percent, at least 40 percent of the lot stays landscaped open space, and the house tops out at 9.5 metres, or 7.5 metres with a flat roof.

Outside Innisfil's settlement areas the standards loosen in one direction and tighten in another. On a shoreline lot under 7,900 square metres, Table 5.8 sets a 30-metre waterfront yard, a 15-metre front yard for the dwelling, 15 percent lot coverage and 60 percent landscaped open space.

On an Innisfil shoreline lot, those numbers decide where the foundation goes before an architect decides what sits on it. The waterfront yard fixes how close the lake-side wall can come to the water, which on a lot that falls toward the lake usually fixes where a walkout can be. The coverage limit fixes the footprint. The height limit, set against the grade you start from, pushes more of the house into a full-height lower level. That is where an ICF basement earns its place: the lower level is insulated on both faces from the day it is poured and takes drywall directly, so it is living space rather than storage.

Where the Lake Simcoe Protection Plan comes in

The Class 1 route is the simplest for another reason. Once an application needs any variation, the by-law lists criteria the Town has to be satisfied on before it issues the permit, and one of them is consistency with the Lake Simcoe Protection Plan. The wording is strong. Where any plan, policy or document conflicts, the provision that gives the greatest protection to the ecological health of the Lake Simcoe watershed prevails.

On an Innisfil shoreline lot, a varied application also has to show that it is compatible with the naturally occurring shoreline, that it includes erosion, runoff and sedimentation control where appropriate, that it uses a servicing solution that does not affect the health of the lake, and that it substantially keeps the natural vegetation along the lot lines, the waterfront yard and the shoreline.

Most of that is settled during excavation, which is the stage of a build nearest to what the Town is protecting. The footprint is opened, spoil comes out, and the footings go in. On our Teardown & Rebuild we sequenced the footing and foundation work tightly so the site spent as little time open as possible. Beside the lake in Innisfil, that discipline matters more, and it is the part of the job our crews do themselves.

Separately, the Town's building permits page notes that many Innisfil properties are regulated by the Lake Simcoe Region Conservation Authority or the Nottawasaga Valley Conservation Authority, and that a regulated property needs written clearance from the right authority before the building permit application goes in.

Away from the lake: the standard Innisfil permit

For an Innisfil lot outside Alcona and off the shoreline, the zoning by-law governs, and the Town advises checking it for size restrictions before applying. The building permit itself goes through Cloudpermit, the online system the Town uses for applications, payments and inspection requests, and the Town states that for most residential projects its review timeline is 10 business days on a complete submission.

Innisfil's checklist for a new single family dwelling is specific about what a complete submission is, and several items land on our side of the job:

  • A lot grading and site servicing plan with a zoning summary, sealed by an Ontario professional engineer or Ontario land surveyor
  • A section through the house showing the size and type of the footing and foundation wall, and the foundation drainage
  • Material and size of every floor, wall and ceiling assembly on the plans
  • An SB-12 Energy Efficiency Design Summary and heat loss and ventilation calculations
  • Conservation authority approval, where it applies

An ICF house shows its difference on that list in two places: the foundation wall section, and the energy summary, where a continuous insulated concrete wall has to be modelled properly. Both are worth getting right before an Innisfil submission rather than in a round of comments.

Custom Home Builder Innisfil — FAQs

Does my Innisfil lot fall under the Community Planning Permit By-law?

It does if it is in Alcona or abuts Lake Simcoe. By-law 058-25 covers all lands within Alcona and all parcels of land abutting Lake Simcoe. Elsewhere in Innisfil, the zoning by-law applies.

Do I still need a building permit after the planning permit?

Yes. The by-law says that after the planning permit is issued and its conditions are met, the owner may apply for a building permit. The Town takes building permit applications through Cloudpermit.

How long does the planning permit take?

The by-law says the Town confirms within five business days whether an application is complete, and decides a complete application within 45 days. A pre-consultation meeting comes first unless the Town says it is not needed.

How far back from the lake does a new house have to be?

Within a settlement area, Table 5.7 sets a 15-metre waterfront yard on a lot 60 metres deep or less, and 30 metres from the water's edge on a deeper lot. Outside settlement areas the waterfront yard is 30 metres.

Will my neighbours be notified?

Only for a Class 3 permit. Then the Town gives notice to every owner within 120 metres and a sign goes on the street and lake sides of the property. Class 1 and Class 2 need no notice.

Are you based in Innisfil?

No. We are based in Barrie, and Innisfil is one of the Simcoe County towns on our published service area.
