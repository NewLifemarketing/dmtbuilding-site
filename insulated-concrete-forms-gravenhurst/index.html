First, what we are and are not

Some people searching insulated concrete forms Gravenhurst are looking for somewhere to buy the block. We are not that. DMT Building Group is an installer and builder: our crews set the forms, brace and align them, place the steel and pour the concrete. We are certified installers on four systems, Nudura, Amvic, Element and Fox, and the forms on your job are whichever one suits it.

If you want the foundation built, rather than the material delivered, read on. We are based in Barrie, Ontario, and Gravenhurst is on the list of Muskoka towns we publish as our service area. Below-grade work is the bulk of what our crews do, on level lots and on stepped rock alike.

And if you are building in Gravenhurst, there is one document the Town has published that shapes a foundation more than any brochure from a block manufacturer. It is about the cold.

Gravenhurst's own cold-weather concrete rules

The Town of Gravenhurst's building department lists a short guide called Cold Weather Requirements for Concrete among its permit resources, written as guidelines under the Ontario Building Code. It starts with a trigger: the requirements apply whenever the air temperature is below 5°C, which can mean a cold morning in May as much as a week in January.

When they apply, the Gravenhurst guide sets out four things:

  • Concrete kept between 10°C and 25°C while it is mixed and placed
  • Once poured, concrete kept at 10°C or warmer for 72 hours
  • No frozen material or ice mixed into concrete or mortar
  • Mortar, masonry and mixing ingredients kept at 5°C or warmer during installation and for at least 48 hours after

It then moves to the site. Insulated tarps or blankets, hay or other methods should keep the undisturbed soil from freezing. Whatever heating is used should extend past the forms to protect the excavation. Water and organic material left by melted snow and ice must be cleared out of the forms. Fresh concrete has to be protected from freezing until it has cured, and Gravenhurst recommends a cold-weather additive in the mix.

What a Gravenhurst footing inspection will not pass

The part of the Gravenhurst guide that decides schedules is the last column, about inspection. When you call for a footing inspection, the forms and any rebar have to be substantially complete. While cold-weather requirements are in effect, the footing forms should be protected before the inspection, and at the inspection they have to be uncovered or easy to see at any point.

Then Gravenhurst says what will fail. An inspector will not approve the inspection if the undisturbed soil appears frozen, if there is ice in the forms, or if the work is not substantially complete. And one line that matters on a lot of Gravenhurst land: forms on bedrock must be kept free of snow and ice.

In Gravenhurst a failed footing inspection is not just a return visit. Gravenhurst asks for 48 hours' notice to book an inspection, through Cloudpermit or by phone, so a failure costs at least two days with the excavation open and the blankets back on. On a winter site that is two more days of heat and protection on the bill. The way to avoid it is to treat the inspection as part of the cold-weather plan, not something that happens after it.

Where insulated concrete forms help in a Gravenhurst winter, and where they do not

An ICF wall is poured into forms made of foam, and the forms stay on. The moment the concrete goes in, it is wrapped on both faces in continuous insulation, which is the protection a bare wall would need blankets and hoarding to get. The concrete's own curing heat is held in. That is a real advantage against the Gravenhurst 72-hour requirement, and it is the reason we can keep raising walls through a Muskoka winter.

It is not the whole answer, and anyone selling it as one is overselling. Footings are not poured inside foam, and they sit on the ground the Town wants kept unfrozen. Slabs and any exposed top surface need conventional protection. The ground rules in the Gravenhurst guide apply in full whatever the walls are made of. One more line in the Town of Gravenhurst guide is worth noticing if you are comparing ICF with a block foundation. Its masonry rules, mortar kept at 5°C for 48 hours and the top of unfinished masonry covered whenever work stops, apply to block walls. An ICF wall is poured concrete, so it falls under the concrete rules instead, with the forms already doing part of the work.

Should you start in winter?

Being straight about it: winter work costs more. Heated mix, additives, blankets, hoarding, fuel and snow clearing are real line items, and a storm still stops a day. What winter buys is a season. A foundation poured in February is a foundation the framers can start on in spring, instead of a hole waiting for the ground to thaw.

On a Gravenhurst lot, whether that trade is worth making depends on access as much as anything. A pump truck needs a plowed, solid pad, and a lake lot down a seasonal road is a different site in February than in October. We will tell you which side of that line your Gravenhurst lot is on after one site walk. Insulated Concrete Forms Gravenhurst — FAQs

Do you sell insulated concrete forms?

No. We are installers and builders, certified on Nudura, Amvic, Element and Fox. We form, brace and pour ICF foundations and walls.

When do Gravenhurst's cold-weather concrete rules apply?

When the air temperature is below 5°C. Concrete must then be kept between 10°C and 25°C while mixed and placed, and at 10°C or more for 72 hours after placing.

Why would a footing inspection fail in winter?

The Town's guide says an inspector will not approve it if the undisturbed soil appears frozen, there is ice in the forms, or the work is not substantially complete. Forms on bedrock must be kept free of snow and ice.

Does ICF remove the need for winter protection?

For the walls, the foam forms provide much of it, because they stay on and insulate both faces. Footings, slabs and exposed tops still need conventional protection, and the ground must not freeze.

How much notice does Gravenhurst need for an inspection?

The Town asks for 48 hours' notice, booked through Cloudpermit or by phone, with the permit number, address, inspection type and requested date.

Are you based in Gravenhurst?

No. We are based in Barrie, and Gravenhurst is one of the Muskoka towns on our published service area.
