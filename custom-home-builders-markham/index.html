$172,307, and the four years that decide how much of it you pay

That figure is the total development charge on one new single or semi-detached house in the City of Markham, from the City's rate sheet effective July 1, 2026. It is the sum of six lines: the City's own hard and soft service charges, York Region's hard and general charges, and the two school boards' education charges. The City collects all of it, and all of it is due before a building permit is issued.

For anyone weighing custom home builders Markham can offer on a lot that already has a house on it, that number changes the conversation. Markham's pamphlet says that where an existing dwelling is demolished, a credit against the charges on the redevelopment may apply, but only where the redevelopment happens within 48 months of the date the demolition approval was obtained, with documented proof.

Forty-eight months sounds generous. It is less so once you add up design, the City's grading review, a heritage permit if the lot is in a district, the building permit and the build itself. A custom home in Markham is a race against a clock that starts the day the old house's demolition is approved.

We are DMT Building Group, based in Barrie, Ontario, and we build across the Greater Toronto Area. Markham is not on the list of towns we name on our site, and we will not pretend to be a Markham company. What we do is build custom homes in insulated concrete form (ICF), with our own crews on the footings, foundation and walls, and we have built the new foundation on a tight infill teardown before.

Where the money goes on a new Markham house

Three things follow from that chart. First, York Region's share is almost half of the total, and the Region's by-law is separate from the City's, so the credit rules are worth confirming with both. Second, the City's rates are indexed every January 1 and July 1, so a house that reaches its permit a year later than planned may pay a different number. Third, Markham also has area-specific charges, by-laws 2022-51 to 2022-72, in places such as South Unionville, Markham Centre and the Future Urban Area, charged per hectare on top of the unit rates. Check the City's area map for your lot.

None of that is our money or our decision. We put it on the page because it is the biggest single number in most Markham rebuilds that is not the house itself, and because the one lever an owner has over it, the timing of the demolition, is a construction scheduling question.

What has to clear before the new house can start

Taking down a house over 10 m² needs its own demolition permit, according to the City's building permit process page. After that, a new house on an infill lot in Markham meets one review that an addition might avoid and a new subdivision home would not see at all.

  • Residential Infill Grading and Servicing, Type I. The City's RGS process applies to new detached infill residential construction of any size that involves grading or servicing work. There is no small-house exemption. The application goes in through ePLAN with a grading and servicing plan and, as the lot requires, a topographic survey, tree report, utility locates, a stormwater management brief, TRCA clearance and a CCTV inspection of the sewer connection. Engineering allows up to 30 days per review cycle, and RGS acceptance has to come before the building permit application.
  • A heritage permit, in four parts of the city. Thornhill, Markham Village, Unionville and Buttonville are heritage conservation districts. There, a new residential dwelling needs a Major Heritage Permit, demolition needs heritage approval as well, most Major applications are reviewed by the Heritage Markham Committee, and a building permit cannot be submitted until the heritage permit is issued.
  • Trees. Markham's Tree Preservation By-law 2023-164 protects private trees with a diameter of 20 cm or more at breast height. Put every protected tree on the survey before the footprint is drawn, not after the excavator arrives.
  • The building permit itself. Through ePLAN, with lot grading approval, a site plan carrying a zoning summary under Comprehensive Zoning By-law 2024-19, finished floor and basement elevations, foundation and footing design, and heat loss and gain calculations. The City's first-review target for a complete house application is 10 business days.

The practical point for a Markham lot: demolition approval should be the last thing you get before you are ready to move fast, not the first. Plan the new house, line up the grading engineer and, in a heritage district, start the heritage conversation while the old house is still standing. A demolition approved early and a design that takes three years to settle is the slowest and most expensive way to build here.

Why an infill rebuild suits a builder who pours its own concrete

Once the approvals are in hand, the clock is still running, and the part of the job most exposed to it is the hole in the ground. A rebuild lot in Markham is usually a lot between neighbours: the old foundation comes out, the new footings go in, and nothing else can happen until they are done.

That is the work we do ourselves. Our Teardown & Rebuild project was an urban infill lot between existing homes: old structure out, new engineered footings and foundation formed within the streetscape, sequenced so the site sat open for as short a time as possible. The same crew that forms the footings raises the ICF walls, so there is no gap in the schedule waiting for a second trade to arrive.

We also keep working through the winter. A demolition approved in the fall does not have to mean a site sitting idle until spring with the 48-month clock ticking; our post on the winter concrete pour explains how a pour is protected in the cold.

Custom Home Builders Markham — FAQs

How much are development charges on a new house in Markham?

The City's rate sheet effective July 1, 2026 totals $172,307 for a single or semi-detached house, including York Region and school board charges. Area-specific charges may apply on top, and rates are indexed every January and July.

Do I get a credit for the house I am tearing down?

Possibly. Markham's pamphlet says a credit may apply where a dwelling is demolished, but only if the redevelopment happens within 48 months of the demolition approval, with documented proof. Confirm with the City's Development Finance department.

What is an RGS application?

Residential Infill Grading and Servicing is the City of Markham's review of grading and servicing on infill lots. A new detached infill house of any size needs a Type I RGS application, accepted before the building permit application.

Can I build a new house in Unionville or Thornhill?

Both are heritage conservation districts, with Markham Village and Buttonville. A new dwelling there needs a Major Heritage Permit, demolition needs heritage approval too, and the building permit cannot be submitted until the heritage permit is issued.

Can you build from my own plans?

Yes. Most conventional plans convert to ICF. We review the drawings and flag anything that needs an engineer before construction.

Are you based in Markham?

No. DMT Building Group is based in Barrie and builds across the Greater Toronto Area, with our own crews on the concrete.
