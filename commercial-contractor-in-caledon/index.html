In Caledon, the first review is about what happens on the land

Most owners looking for a commercial contractor in Caledon expect the first hard questions to be about the building: its height, its setbacks, its structure. On a good share of Caledon sites the first hard questions come from somewhere else. Peel Region asks what you will keep on the property, what you will spread on the parking lot in January, and how deep you will dig.

Those questions sit on a Region of Peel form under Section 59 of the Clean Water Act, 2006. It applies where a development proposal falls inside a vulnerable area around a municipal drinking water source, and it covers new construction, expansions, alterations and changes of use. Where the Section 59 policies apply, the form says a written Notice from the Risk Management Official is required before a building permit is issued. The Town of Caledon lists source water protection, where applicable, among the other approvals on its permit checklist for a new industrial, commercial or institutional building.

We are DMT Building Group, an insulated concrete form (ICF) specialist based in Barrie, Ontario. Caledon is not one of the towns named on our published service area, which lists the Greater Toronto Area towns we work in most; we build across the GTA from Barrie, and we will tell you plainly whether a Caledon site is one we can take on. What we do on a commercial job is the concrete: footings, foundations and full-height ICF walls.

What Peel's Section 59 screening asks a Caledon site

The form is long, and most of it is about the business, not the building. For a commercial construction project in Caledon it asks, among other things, about:

  • Liquid fuel and fuel oil storage, with the largest quantity held on site at one time
  • Dense non-aqueous phase liquids in any quantity, and organic solvents by volume, with degreasers, paint thinners, adhesives and dry cleaning fluid named as examples
  • Road salt on parking lots and walkways, given as total paved area or number of parking spaces, plus salt and snow storage
  • Stormwater facilities that discharge to land or surface water, and industrial effluent
  • New impervious surface: the paved area and the building footprint, both in square metres
  • Wells, boreholes, geothermal systems and septic systems on the property
  • Any excavation, regrading, ditching, or new sewer and water lines

Read that list as a builder and three items are about the foundation contract directly. The building footprint is the slab. The excavation question is the dig for the footings. The servicing question is the trench that brings water and sewer into the foundation wall. The form's own appendix explains why digging matters: a human-made feature that disturbs the soil and rock above an aquifer can become what the Clean Water Act calls a transport pathway, and Peel has to be notified of one.

So the numbers on that form should come from the people who will actually do the digging. On a Caledon site we can give your designer the footprint, the excavation depth and extent, and the service entry locations as they will be built, not as a guess made before anyone has looked at the soils report.

Four outcomes, and only one is a straight yes

The form is meant to go in before the application under the Planning Act or the Building Code Act, with a site plan, a sketch or design drawings attached. The Risk Management Official follows up within ten business days to say whether more information is needed or whether the file can proceed. A proposal that needs a risk management plan goes into further consultation before any Notice is issued, and that is time that has to sit in the Caledon schedule before site plan or permit review can finish.

The practical point for a Caledon owner is order. If the business that will occupy the building handles fuel, solvents or large paved areas, settle the screening early, because a design that has to change to satisfy a risk management plan is cheaper to change before the footings are drawn than after.

Then the use sets the charge

Caledon's development charge sheet effective August 1, 2026 splits non-residential floor area into two classes. Industrial pays $379.05 per square metre of gross floor area: $120.83 to the Town of Caledon, $242.07 to the Region of Peel and $16.15 in education charges. Everything else non-residential pays $453.64, and the whole difference sits in the Region's portion, which rises to $316.66. The Town's own share is the same for both.

On a 2,000 square metre building that gap is about $149,000, decided by a word on the application. The same sheet notes that while residential charges for non-rental housing moved to occupancy on November 3, 2025, non-residential charges in Caledon, industrial and other alike, are still payable when the building permit is issued. The Town and Peel rates are indexed again on February 1, 2027 unless Council directs otherwise, so confirm the rate in force on the day your Caledon permit will actually issue.

The use you declare is therefore read twice in Caledon: once by the Region's source water screening, for what the business will handle, and once by the charge sheet, for what the floor area is for. A Land and Building Use Declaration Form is also on the Town's ICI permit checklist. They should all tell the same story, and it should be the true one.

Commercial construction in Caledon, from screening to securities

The Town of Caledon designates all land inside its limits as a site plan control area, and its site plan page says commercial, industrial and institutional projects go through site plan approval. Properties on the Oak Ridges Moraine follow a separate process set out on the Town's moraine page. The site plan file does not end when the building permit issues.

Step five is the one that ties the construction to the paperwork. After the project is complete, Caledon asks for engineering and landscape certifications, signed and stamped by accredited professionals, along with a landscape plant warranty certification, before securities are released, and the Town inspects the site. An engineer certifies what was built. If the grading, the drainage or the building's position drift from the approved site plan during construction, that is where it shows, with the owner's money still held.

The Town's checklist for a new ICI building adds the pieces the structure is checked against: a site plan showing final grading, building height and setbacks fully dimensioned with water and sanitary lines located and sized, a foundation plan, and structural drawings with the Caledon Climatic Design Loads indicated. It also asks for the Supplementary Standard Form for SB-10 compliance and a general review commitment signed by all professionals and the owner.

Commercial Contractor in Caledon — FAQs

Does every commercial building in Caledon need a Section 59 notice?

No. It applies where the site is inside a vulnerable area around a municipal drinking water source and the Section 59 policies apply. Where they do, Peel's form says a written Notice from the Risk Management Official is needed before a building permit is issued.

Is all of Caledon under site plan control?

Yes. The Town designates all land within its limits as a site plan control area, and commercial, industrial and institutional projects go through site plan approval. Oak Ridges Moraine properties follow a separate process.

What are the development charges on an industrial building in Caledon?

On the Town's sheet effective August 1, 2026, industrial floor area pays $379.05 per square metre and other non-residential floor area pays $453.64, combining Town, Region of Peel and education charges.

When are non-residential charges paid in Caledon?

At building permit issuance. The Town's sheet notes that non-rental residential charges moved to occupancy, but industrial and other non-residential charges are still payable when the permit is issued.

How are site plan securities released?

After completion, Caledon asks for engineering and landscape certifications signed and stamped by accredited professionals, plus a landscape plant warranty certification, and the Town inspects the site before securities are released.

Are you based in Caledon?

No. We are based in Barrie and build across the Greater Toronto Area. Caledon is not on our published list of towns, so send us the site and we will tell you plainly whether we can take it on.
