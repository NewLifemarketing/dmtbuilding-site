In Oshawa, the first number is set by the use

Two buildings of the same size, on similar lots, in the same city, can carry very different bills before a footing is dug. For Oshawa commercial construction the difference starts with one word on the application: commercial, industrial or institutional.

The City of Oshawa's Development Charges By-law 91-2024 exempts any new industrial building, and the enlargement of an existing industrial building, from the City's own development charge. A commercial or institutional building pays the City $201.98 per square metre at the rates in effect from July 1, 2026. The Region of Durham charges all three, at three different rates. All of it is collected by Oshawa's Building Services branch, and for most non-residential work it is due on or before the day the building permit is issued.

We are DMT Building Group, an ICF specialist based in Barrie, Ontario. We build across the Greater Toronto Area; Oshawa is not one of the towns named on our published service-area list, so we will be plain: we have no office in Oshawa, and we travel to the site. On commercial work we deliver the concrete scope, footings, foundations and full-height insulated concrete form walls, either under your general contractor or carried through to the above-grade walls.

Read the Oshawa chart for what it shows, not for a budget. On this example the gap between commercial and industrial is about $437,000 in development charges alone. It comes from a definition, not from anything poured. So the use has to be settled, and agreed with the City, before anyone prices the walls.

Oshawa's industrial exemption also does not make the building free of charges. The Region's industrial rate, the two school board rates and, outside a subdivision, parkland cash-in-lieu still apply. Oshawa sets non-residential cash-in-lieu at 2% of property value for commercial or industrial land and 5% for other non-residential uses, and it applies only outside subdivisions.

When the City collects, and what fixes the rate

Oshawa's page is direct: non-residential development charges are payable upon issuance of a building permit. Residential charges may be deferred to occupancy; commercial and industrial ones are not. Institutional development is the exception on the non-residential side, paid in six equal annual instalments starting at occupancy.

For a commercial owner in Oshawa that changes the cash flow of the whole job. The development charges land at the same moment as mobilisation, excavation and the first concrete invoices. A builder who prices the structure without that line is pricing half the opening month.

The rate itself moves twice a year: Oshawa indexes on January 1 and July 1. There is one way to hold it still: for permits where a site plan agreement or zoning by-law amendment was applied for after January 1, 2020, the City says City and Region rates will be frozen, and interest may be added. The date your site plan application goes in can fix the number your permit pays. That is a reason to treat the site plan filing as a budget decision, not just a planning one.

Location can change the figure too. The City's own charge does not apply on lands wholly within the core area of the Downtown Oshawa Urban Growth Centre, and Oshawa runs community improvement incentives in four areas: Brownfields Renaissance, Harbour Road, the Urban Growth Centre, and Wentworth Street West. Check the lot against each before the pro forma is final.

Commercial construction in Oshawa, in the City's order

Oshawa's site plan control applies to commercial, institutional and industrial buildings, and the City states that where site plan approval is required, building cannot begin until it is given. Pre-consultation is strongly recommended, and the City suggests calling Planning Services first about current zoning and possible road widening. Stage 1 returns comments from City departments and outside agencies on what plans, studies and reports a complete application needs.

At the Oshawa permit stage, drawings for non-residential buildings must be signed, sealed and dated by an architect, engineer or qualified designer. The applicable-law list then adds the outside agencies: a Ministry of Transportation building and land use permit within 400 metres of Highway 401 or 407, and Central Lake Ontario Conservation where the work is in a fill-regulated area or flood plain or may interfere with a watercourse.

One detail matters to anyone in a hurry. If an owner wants grading to start before the site plan agreement is signed, Oshawa has a pre-servicing permit under its Site Alteration By-law, and the submission is substantial: engineering drawings, a preliminary grading plan, erosion and sediment control, a haul route plan, a soils management plan, a security deposit and a certificate of insurance.

Oshawa Commercial Construction — FAQs

Does Oshawa charge development charges on industrial buildings?

Not the City's own charge. By-law 91-2024 exempts a new industrial building and the enlargement of an existing one. The Region of Durham's industrial rate, $19.80 per square foot as of July 1, 2026, and school board charges still apply.

When are commercial development charges paid in Oshawa?

On issuance of the building permit. Residential charges may be deferred to occupancy, and institutional charges are paid in six annual instalments from occupancy, but commercial and industrial charges are due at issuance.

Does a commercial building in Oshawa need site plan approval?

Yes. The City lists commercial, institutional and industrial buildings among the projects that need it, and states that building cannot begin until approval is given.

Can the development charge rate be locked?

Where a site plan agreement or zoning by-law amendment was applied for after January 1, 2020, the City says City and Region rates will be frozen, and interest may be added. Confirm with your plans examiner.

Have you built commercial projects in Oshawa?

No. Our published commercial work is the Pebble Ridge foundation package and an indoor sports facility build-out in Owen Sound. We are based in Barrie and build across the Greater Toronto Area.

Can you work under our general contractor?

Yes. We regularly carry the concrete and ICF scope for builders and general contractors, or take it through to the above-grade walls.
