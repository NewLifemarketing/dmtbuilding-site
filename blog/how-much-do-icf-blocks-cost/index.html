How Much Do ICF Blocks Cost

Price the Wall, Not the Block

A block is a size, not an amount of wall. Divide the price by what it covers, then remember the block is the smallest line on the quote.

How much do ICF blocks cost? On the day this was written, one building-supply website listed a Fox Blocks 6-inch straight block at $46.99. That is a real number and you can check it. It is also close to useless on its own, because an ICF block is not a fixed amount of wall. Blocks from different systems are different sizes, a corner is not priced like a straight run, and the block is the cheapest thing in the wall it becomes.

So here is the number, what it means once you divide it properly, and where it sits on an actual quote.

  • What One Block Costs
  • Divide by the Wall It Covers

How Much Do ICF Blocks Cost? What One Block Costs

The listing we used is Kent’s product page for the Fox Blocks 6″ Straight Block, model FOX-5600A: $46.99 each when we read it. Fox’s own sizing sheet gives that block a face 48 inches long and 16 inches high, sold 12 to a bundle.

Three things to keep in mind before you put that number in a spreadsheet:

  • It is a retail price for one block. A full foundation order through a dealer is priced as an order, by the bundle, and is quoted rather than listed.
  • It is one system. Nudura, Amvic, Element and Fox all make blocks, and they are not the same block.
  • It is one day. Foam and plastic prices move, and so does every price list built on them.

Treat it as a reference point, not a budget.

Divide by the Wall It Covers

Any answer to how much do ICF blocks cost only means something once you divide it by the wall the block places. That Fox block covers 48 by 16 inches, which is 5.33 square feet. At $46.99 that works out to about $8.82 per square foot of wall face, before any corners, concrete or steel.

Now take the system we build with most. A Nudura standard form is 96 inches long and 18 inches high, a figure Nudura publishes in its product catalogue and that ICC-ES evaluation report ESR-2092 confirms. One Nudura form places 12 square feet of wall. That is 2.25 times the wall of a 48-by-16 block.

So a Nudura form will always carry a bigger price per block, and that says nothing at all about whether the wall costs more. Comparing two systems block for block is like comparing two lumber prices without asking how long the boards are. Put every quote you get on the same footing:

  • Price per block, divided by the square feet that block places.
  • The same core width on both sides of the comparison.
  • The same pieces counted: straights, corners and any special forms.

The block size also changes the count. Our post on how many ICF blocks you need walks through counting an order course by course, and the ICF block dimensions post covers what each number on a Nudura spec sheet turns into on site.

The Blocks That Are Not Straight

The straight block is most of the wall. Fox’s own product description says it typically makes up 85% of the ICF wall assembly on most residential and commercial jobs. The rest is the part a per-block price quietly skips.

Every corner of every course takes a corner form. A 45-degree turn takes a 45. A wall that meets another wall in the middle of a run takes a T. Where brick or stone sits on the foundation, a brick-ledge form carries it. Where floor joists or a sill plate need a wider bearing at the top of the wall, a taper-top form opens out the core. Nudura lists each of these in its catalogue as its own form, and on a price list each one is its own line.

None of those pieces places as much wall as a straight block, so every one of them raises the cost of each square foot it covers. A plain rectangle has four corners a course. A house with bump-outs, a garage jog and a bay window can have twice that. Two houses with the same wall area can need noticeably different money in blocks, and the difference is the outline, not the square footage.

How Much Do ICF Blocks Cost on a Complicated Plan?

More per square foot, and for two reasons. The special pieces cost more, and the cut blocks around steps, angles and openings leave offcuts that still had to be bought. The bigger number on a complicated plan, though, is not the blocks at all. It is the labour. Corners, steps, curves and changes in height all cost labour. A straightforward rectangle goes up at roughly 100 linear feet a day. A radius wall does not.

What the Core Width Does to the Price

The core is the space between the two foam faces, where the concrete goes. Nudura makes forms for 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12-inch cores. Every one of them carries the same foam: two panels of 2 5/8 inches each. A wider block is not a warmer block. It is a stronger wall.

Wider forms are listed as separate items and priced separately, but the block is not where a wider core really costs you. The concrete is. A 6-inch core holds half a cubic foot of concrete for every square foot of wall. An 8-inch core holds two-thirds. Across 1,000 square feet of wall, that is about 18.5 cubic yards against about 24.7: six more yards of concrete on the truck, more weight on the footing, and often more steel. The engineer sets the core from the wall height, the backfill and the load, and that decision moves the price of the wall more than the price of the block does.

What the Block Price Leaves Out

A block on a pallet is not a wall. Between the delivery and a finished foundation, the wall needs:

  • Concrete, and the pump to place it.
  • Reinforcing steel, horizontal in the webs and vertical in the cores.
  • Bracing and alignment, to hold the forms plumb and straight through the pour.
  • Bucks at every window and door opening.
  • The crew that stacks it, braces it, pours it and checks it.

Every one of those is a bigger line than the blocks. That is why a quote built up from the per-block price always comes out low. It is the same lesson as the R-value: the number on the label is only half the story.

It also works the other way. An ICF wall arrives having done several jobs that a framed wall pays separately for: no interior framing, no batt, no vapour barrier, no strapping, and three trades off the critical path. Our post on what ICF actually costs sets that comparison out against wood frame for the whole build.

How Much Do ICF Blocks Cost on Your House? How We Price It

We quote every job off the drawings and the site, and the honest answer on our foundation pricing is that it turns on four things, none of which is the block:

  • The site. Stepped footings, scribing to bedrock and machine access move the number more than any other single factor.
  • The shape. Corners, steps, curves and changes in height, as above.
  • How you engage us. Supply and install is the usual arrangement: we provide the ICF and install it. If you are a builder who already buys forms, install-only is a different line entirely: you supply the forms, we set and pour them.
  • What is in scope. Footings, frost walls, slabs, waterproofing, drainage and floor assemblies can each be in or out. Two quotes drawing that line in different places is the most common reason two numbers look far apart when the work is nearly identical.

We itemize all of it, so you can see which side each line sits on. We are also not tied to one block. Our installers are certified on Nudura, Amvic, Element and Fox, and we will recommend the right block for your project’s engineering and budget, or build with the system you pick.

FAQ

What does a single ICF block cost?

One example: Kent listed the Fox Blocks 6-inch straight block at $46.99 when this was written. It covers 5.33 square feet of wall, so that is about $8.82 per square foot of wall face, before corners, concrete, steel or labour.

Why do some ICF blocks cost much more than others?

Mostly because they are bigger. A Nudura standard form is 96 by 18 inches and places 12 square feet of wall; a 48 by 16-inch block places 5.33. Compare blocks by price per square foot of wall, at the same core width, not by price per block.

Do corner ICF blocks cost more?

For the wall they place, yes. Corners, 45s, T-forms, brick-ledge and taper-top forms are separate items, and none of them covers as much wall per piece as a straight block. A plan with more corners and steps needs more of them, and more labour.

Does a wider ICF block cost more?

The form for a wider core is priced separately, but the bigger difference is the concrete. An 8-inch core holds a third more concrete than a 6-inch core for the same wall. The foam, and so the insulation, is the same on every core width.

Can I buy the ICF blocks myself and hire a crew to install them?

Yes. Supply and install is the usual arrangement, but we also work install-only: you supply the forms, we set and pour them. It is priced as a different line entirely.
