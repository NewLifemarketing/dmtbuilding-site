Do ICF Foundations Need Waterproofing?

ICF foundations shrug off moisture better than bare concrete — but do they still need waterproofing? A DMT builder explains what below-grade ICF really requires.

Short answer: yes. An ICF foundation resists moisture far better than a bare concrete wall, but the foam does not make the assembly waterproof on its own. Below grade, an ICF foundation still needs proper damp-proofing, waterproofing and drainage — the same water-management job every foundation has, done to suit the wall.

People assume the two layers of foam seal everything out. They don't. The foam insulates and protects the concrete; keeping water away from the wall is a separate job, and skipping it is how you end up with a "warm" basement that still smells like a basement.

We'll cover:

  • What ICF already does for you below grade
  • What still needs waterproofing and drainage
  • How the ICF-specific detailing differs
  • How DMT details a dry ICF basement

What ICF already does for you below grade

Start with what you're not fighting anymore. An ICF wall is a solid, reinforced concrete core wrapped in continuous foam on both faces. Two things follow from that:

  • The concrete is monolithic. Poured in controlled lifts, there are no block joints or cold seams for water to track through the way it can in a concrete-block wall.
  • The exterior foam protects the concrete and moves the dew point outside the structural core, so the inside face stays warm and dry instead of sweating.

That's a real head start. It's why an ICF basement can be finished living space from day one instead of a cold, damp storage pit. But "resists moisture" is not "keeps out standing water and hydrostatic pressure." Rain, snowmelt and a high water table still push against the outside of that wall every spring.

What still needs waterproofing and drainage

Below grade, you're managing water in two ways, and ICF changes neither requirement:

  1. A moisture barrier on the wall. Damp-proofing (a coating that resists soil moisture) or full waterproofing (a membrane that holds back liquid water under pressure) goes on the exterior face. Which one depends on your site — a flat lot with good soil is a different problem than a wall sitting in clay or below the water table.
  2. Drainage that gets water away from the footing. This is the "stone and weeper" stage: free-draining granular backfill and a weeping-tile (perimeter drain) system at the footing that carries water to daylight or a sump before it ever loads the wall.

Ontario's Building Code requires below-grade foundation walls enclosing habitable space to be dampproofed — and fully waterproofed where soils drain poorly or the water table is high — with a perimeter drainage system at the footing, per the Construction Codes of Ontario. ICF doesn't exempt you from that — the wall assembly is different, so the detailing is adapted, but the water still has to be stopped and drained.

How the ICF-specific detailing differs

The principles are the same as any foundation; the execution is tuned to the foam:

  • The waterproofing membrane or coating has to be compatible with the EPS foam face — some solvent-based products attack foam, so you match the product to the substrate.
  • Terminations at the top of the wall, at window bucks and at service penetrations need to be detailed so water can't get behind the membrane.
  • The drainage layer and weeping tile are sized and placed the same way, tied into the footing — which is exactly why footings and foundation get planned together, not as separate jobs.

Get those right and the reinforced core plus continuous insulation protects against frost, shifting temperatures and long-term wear — a wall built to stay dry, not just to look dry on handover day.

FAQ

Is an ICF foundation waterproof on its own?

No. It's highly moisture-resistant — monolithic concrete with continuous foam and no block joints — but it still needs exterior damp-proofing or waterproofing plus proper drainage below grade to keep liquid water and hydrostatic pressure off the wall.

What's the difference between damp-proofing and waterproofing?

Damp-proofing is a coating that resists soil moisture and vapour; waterproofing is a membrane that holds back liquid water under pressure. Wetter sites, clay soils, or walls below the water table call for full waterproofing rather than just damp-proofing.

Do ICF basements leak?

Not when they're detailed properly. Leaks come from skipped or poorly terminated moisture barriers and missing drainage — not from ICF itself. Pair the wall with the right membrane and a working weeping-tile system and it stays dry.
