Three dates that set what a Toronto building costs

When you line up commercial builders Toronto offers, the natural thing to compare is the price of the building. The City of Toronto adds a second set of numbers that depend less on what you build than on when things happen: when the site plan application was deemed complete, when the first building permit is issued, and whether you enrolled in a refund program before the planners issued their conditions.

None of these are construction dates, and that is the point. By the time a contractor is pricing walls, most of them have already passed. This page walks through them in order, because the wall assembly you pick, including whether it is insulated concrete, gets decided inside that calendar.

Date one: the site plan application

Development charges in Toronto are set by By-law 1137-2022, enacted August 15, 2022. For non-residential development the charge is based on floor area. The rate that applies is not always the rate on the day you pay, though. The City's overview sets out a test: if a site plan application was submitted on or after January 1, 2020, if no more than 18 months pass between its approval and the first building permit, and if the charge at the planning-application rate plus statutory interest comes out lower than the charge at the permit rate, then the planning-application rate applies.

Two practical points follow. The 18-month window, shortened from two years by provincial Bill 185 in June 2024, is a schedule constraint on the whole design team. And the City has said it will stop accruing that interest from the date the building permit is issued. Council also removed annual indexing of the charges for 2025 and 2026, and the City is in a comprehensive review of its development charges expected to run into 2027. The rate you budget against is worth confirming with Toronto Building, not carrying over from a previous job.

Some uses owe nothing. The City's by-law exempts industrial uses as it defines them, and non-residential uses that are not on the ground floor, among others. Provincial legislation exempts the enlargement of an industrial building by up to 50 per cent of the floor area that existed before the by-law came into force.

Date two: the first building permit

For homes, Toronto now collects development charges at occupancy. For commercial and industrial buildings it does not. The City's overview is direct: without a Section 27 agreement, non-residential charges are payable at the time of first building permit issuance. Rental and institutional buildings pay in six equal annual instalments starting at occupancy, and non-rental residential pays at occupancy.

So on commercial construction Toronto expects the charge in full at the permit, before construction starts, on top of the permit fee itself, which for large residential and non-residential buildings is the service index for the occupancy multiplied by the floor area of the work, with a 2026 minimum of $214.79. That is a cash-flow fact that lands on the owner's side of the table, but it shapes the contractor's side too: a project that is paying its charges at permit is a project that wants its structure going up quickly once that permit is in hand.

Date three: before the Notice of Approval Conditions

The Toronto Green Standard is the City's sustainable design and performance requirement for new development, in place since 2010. Version 4 has applied to new planning applications since May 1, 2022. Tier 1 is mandatory and verified through site plan approval; the Version 4 standard for mid- to high-rise residential and non-residential development covers all industrial, commercial and institutional projects. Provincial Bill 98 received Royal Assent on June 2, 2026, and applications submitted after that date follow the City's Interim Version 4 checklists.

The higher tiers are voluntary and pay. Through the Development Charge Refund Program, a project verified at Tier 2 or above gets a partial refund of the development charges it paid. The program's conditions are written in dates:

  • Enrol before the NOAC. The refund application and estimate must be accepted by Environmental Planning before the Notice of Approval Conditions is issued in site plan approval.
  • Hire the evaluator by 50% construction documents. A third-party evaluator registered with the City is retained at the owner's expense by the 50% construction documentation stage.
  • Verify twice. Review of the construction drawings, then a site inspection at occupancy.
  • Keep the clock. Enrolment lapses if charges are not paid and the permit is not issued within a year of the NOAC; the verification report is due within five years of the first above-grade permit.

By the 50% drawing stage the building envelope is mostly decided. Energy modelling for the Standard happens on the design team's side, and we do not promise any tier for a building we have not modelled. What we can do is be in the room early enough that an insulated concrete wall is a real option in that modelling rather than a value-engineering idea that arrives after the drawings are fixed.

What the Toronto permit asks of the structure

Toronto Building's guide for large residential and non-residential permits has a few requirements that land squarely on whoever carries the concrete:

  • A Vibration Control Form for every project that is not Part 9 residential, setting out construction activities that may cause vibration and the preventative measures planned before they start.
  • A Commitment to General Reviews by the architect and engineer, signed with the owner, wherever the Ontario Building Code requires professional design and field review.
  • An energy efficiency design form for a new building or an addition, showing compliance with Supplemental Standard SB-10.
  • A grading plan with existing and proposed grades, sidewalk elevations and the finished floor elevation of every level.
  • A Municipal Road Damage deposit covering the curb, sidewalk, road, laneway and boulevard while work goes on.

One requirement is particular to downtown. Under By-law 1432-2017, enacted in December 2017, land near St. Michael's Hospital and the Hospital for Sick Children is regulated to protect their helipad flight paths, and permit applications in those areas carry additional submission requirements. If your site is in one of those areas, tell everyone pricing the job.

Commercial Builders Toronto — FAQs

When are development charges paid on a commercial building in Toronto?

Without a Section 27 agreement, non-residential development charges are payable when the first building permit is issued. Homes and rental buildings follow different timing.

Can our site plan application lock in a lower charge?

It can. If the site plan application was made on or after January 1, 2020, the first permit follows approval within 18 months, and the planning-application rate plus interest is lower than the permit rate, the City applies the planning-application rate.

Is the Toronto Green Standard mandatory?

Tier 1 is, verified through site plan approval. Tiers 2 to 4 are voluntary, and verified projects can receive a partial development charge refund if they enrol before the Notice of Approval Conditions.

Is an industrial building exempt from development charges?

Toronto's by-law exempts industrial uses as it defines them, and provincial legislation exempts enlarging an industrial building by up to 50 per cent of its original floor area. Confirm your use with Toronto Building.

Do you work as a subcontractor to a GC?

Yes. Much of our work is the concrete and ICF scope for builders and general contractors, handed over backfill-ready or carried through the walls above grade.

Are you a Toronto company?

No. We are based in Barrie and build across the Greater Toronto Area; Toronto is on our published list of GTA cities.
