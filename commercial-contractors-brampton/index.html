No site plan approval, no permit application

Ask any of the commercial contractors Brampton owners call what they want to know first, and the answer is usually the same: when can we start? The City of Brampton answers part of that before any contractor does. Its permit requirements for a new industrial, commercial or institutional building say plainly that building permit applications will not be accepted without site plan approval, and they ask for two sets of the site-plan-approved drawings with the application.

So the clock on commercial construction in Brampton does not start at the building permit. It starts at site plan, under Brampton's Site Plan Control By-law 96-86 and Section 41 of the Ontario Planning Act, which the City applies to commercial, industrial, institutional and multiple residential development. For anyone pricing a structure, that changes what the first useful conversation is about.

Four streams of site plan review

Brampton sorts site plan applications by scale and complexity. Which stream a project lands in decides how much review it gets, and whether a Site Plan Agreement is needed.

  • Excluded. Minor physical changes to a site or building exterior, or work controlled by another review process. The City keeps a published Excluded from Site Plan Control list; if the work is on it, you go straight to the Building Division.
  • Limited. One simple activity, no external agency review, no Site Plan Agreement. Applied for through BramPlanOnline.
  • Basic. Small proposals with a scoped set of technical reviews. A Pre-Application Consultation meeting is encouraged.
  • Full. Everything else: technical review by most internal groups, any number of external agencies, and a new Site Plan Agreement.

Brampton has stopped issuing site plan waivers; the Excluded and Limited streams replaced them. Approval of site plans is delegated by Council to Planning staff, so there is no statutory public meeting in this process. For a Basic or Full application, the Pre-Application Consultation is booked through BramPlanOnline and held on the next available Tuesday afternoon after a preliminary technical review. It produces the checklist of drawings, reports and studies the formal application needs.

The City lists what site plan review weighs: zoning, grading and servicing, transportation and transit, urban design, landscaping, accessibility, environmental conservation, provincial noise, vibration and odour guidelines, and outside agencies, with the Region of Peel, CVC and TRCA each publishing their own requirements. Grading and servicing in particular end up on the same drawings the foundation is built to, which is why we want to see them while they are still being commented on.

The day you apply fixes the charge

This is the part of commercial construction Brampton owners most often find out late. The City's development charges pamphlet says the charge payable is the one determined under the by-law on the day of application for site plan control, or, if there is none, on the day of application for rezoning, or otherwise on the day the by-law sets. Development charges are then paid before the building permit is released.

And the charge depends on what the building is for. Brampton's current sheet, effective August 1, 2026 until January 31, 2027, sets non-residential charges per square metre of total floor area, combining the City, the Region of Peel and the two school boards. Industrial floor area carries the lowest total, $384.80 per square metre. The sheet's two other non-residential categories total $558.24 and $758.96. On top of that, non-residential development pays cash in lieu of parkland: 2% of the property's market value on the day the building permit is issued, appraised by the City's Realty Services.

Two practical points follow. The use you declare in the site plan application is not a detail to settle later; it decides which line of that sheet you are on, and the pamphlet lists specific exemptions, including some changes of use from industrial where the original building predates 2004. And because the rates are indexed every February 1 and August 1, the date of the site plan application has a dollar value. Confirm your own numbers with the City's Development Finance staff; the by-law, not any summary of it, is what applies.

The approvals that come from outside City Hall

Brampton's applicable-law checklist, attached to every commercial permit package, names the outside approvals that must be in hand before a permit can issue. Three of them are worth checking on day one for a Brampton site.

Highways 410 and 407

The checklist flags construction within 45 metres of the road, or within 395 metres of an intersection, on Highways 410 or 407. Those sites need a Ministry of Transportation building and land use permit.

TRCA or CVC

Etobicoke Creek, in TRCA's jurisdiction, flows south through Brampton. CVC regulates work near watercourses, floodplains, valley slopes and wetlands under O. Reg. 41/24. Either may need to issue a permit first.

Record of Site Condition

Where land changes from industrial or commercial to agricultural, residential or park use, the checklist calls for a filed Record of Site Condition under the Environmental Protection Act.

Both zoning by-laws

While By-law 014-2026 is under appeal, Brampton applies it together with 270-2004, and the stricter provision governs.

What the permit package asks of the structure

Once site plan is approved, the City's New ICI Projects package sets out what goes in with the permit application through the Brampton Building and Business Portal. Most of it is the design team's: architectural, structural, mechanical, plumbing and electrical drawings, HVAC calculations, energy forms under Supplementary Standard SB-10 where they apply, and a detailed letter of use stating the operation, the number of employees and the occupant load. Industrial storage or manufacturing uses also describe their processes and the materials stored.

Two items are where we come in. The application needs a soil engineer's report, and a General Review Commitment Certificate, the designers' commitment to review the work during construction. The soils report decides what the footings bear on. General review means the structure we build is checked against the stamped drawings as it goes up. Both suit how we work: we build to the stamp, and we flag anything on site that does not match the drawings.

For any commercial contractor in Brampton, that is the real test. A commercial contractor Brampton owners can rely on is one whose footings and walls pass the designer's review the first time, on a schedule that the planning file has already stretched.

Commercial Contractors Brampton — FAQs

Can I apply for a commercial building permit before site plan approval?

No. Brampton's requirements for new industrial, commercial and institutional buildings state that building permit applications will not be accepted without site plan approval.

When is the development charge on a Brampton commercial building set?

According to the City's pamphlet, the charge is the one under the by-law on the day of application for site plan control, or rezoning if there is no site plan, or otherwise the day the by-law sets. It is paid before the permit is released.

How much are non-residential development charges in Brampton?

The sheet effective August 1, 2026 lists combined totals of $384.80 per square metre for industrial floor area, and $558.24 and $758.96 for the two other non-residential categories, plus cash in lieu of parkland at 2% of market value.

Does a site near Highway 410 or 407 need extra approval?

It can. Brampton's applicable-law checklist flags construction within 45 metres of those highways, or within 395 metres of an intersection, as needing a Ministry of Transportation permit.

Can you work under our general contractor?

Yes. General contractors bring us in for the concrete and ICF scope and take over a backfill-ready foundation, or we carry the structure on through the walls.

Are you based in Brampton?

No. We are based in Barrie and build across the Greater Toronto Area, travelling to Brampton sites for the work.
