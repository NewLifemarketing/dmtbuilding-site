A cottage town on a sandy shore

Wasaga Beach is built behind a long, sandy shore. Ontario Parks describes Wasaga Beach Provincial Park as 7 km of sandy shore across four beach areas That is a very different place to build from cottage country further north. There is no Canadian Shield to pin a footing to. The questions in Wasaga Beach are about water: how high it comes, where it drains, and how the ground around the building is shaped.

A cottage builder Wasaga Beach owners hire may well be building something that is a cottage in name and a house in every other way, if the owners plan to use it in February as well as July, or to live in it one day. So this page treats the two together: a four-season cottage and a custom home in Wasaga Beach face the same ground, the same rules, and the same answer in the foundation.

We are DMT Building Group, based in Barrie, Ontario. Wasaga Beach is on the list of Simcoe County towns we publish as our service area. We build in insulated concrete form (ICF), from the footings up, with our own crews doing the concrete.

What the Town of Wasaga Beach asks of a new dwelling

The Town's residential building page sets out what a new single-family dwelling application needs. Most of it is the usual set of drawings. One item stands out, because it is the one that decides how a Wasaga Beach lot behaves in a storm.

  • A site plan, a foundation plan, and a floor plan for each floor
  • Roof and floor framing packages and layouts
  • Building elevations and building sections
  • A lot grading and drainage plan, stamped by a Certified Engineering Technologist (C.E.T.) or Professional Engineer (P.Eng.)
  • Permits or clearances from other agencies where required, which the Town lists as including the NVCA, the Ministry of Transportation and the Committee of Adjustment

The stamped grading plan is the one to plan around. It fixes the elevations around the house and where surface water goes, and the foundation is built to it. On flat, sandy ground with water nearby, a few hundred millimetres of elevation is the difference between a dry lower level and a wet one, which is why the Town wants a qualified professional to sign it.

Georgian Bay, the river, and the NVCA

The Nottawasaga Valley Conservation Authority's own guidance on natural hazards reads like a description of the Wasaga Beach shoreline. Properties along the Georgian Bay shoreline in its jurisdiction are prone to shoreline flooding: when the bay is high, more of a property is under water, and waves can do serious damage as they reach the higher shore. In dynamic beach areas, NVCA notes, flood hazard zones are unpredictable because elevations change dramatically from season to season and year to year.

NVCA's position on new development follows from that. It requires new development to be located outside areas prone to flooding, and near the river it asks owners to show that grading will not reduce flood storage capacity. For a cottage on or near the water in Wasaga Beach, that means the conservation authority can shape where the building goes and how high it sits, before the Town's permit is in play.

Custom home builder Wasaga Beach owners ask us about: the foundation

If you are comparing custom home builders Wasaga Beach has, or cottage builders, or a custom home design build Wasaga Beach firm, ask each one the same thing: what does the foundation do about water? On a Wasaga Beach lot, where the threat is water rather than rock, the answer should cover the height, the drainage and the wall itself.

The height comes from the stamped grading plan and, near the shoreline or the river, from whatever NVCA requires. We build the footings and foundation to those elevations and nothing else. The drainage is in our scope: weeping tile, interior stone and exterior waterproofing go in as part of the foundation, in the right order. And the wall is insulated concrete, foam on both faces of a reinforced core, so the lower level is a dry, warm part of the house rather than a damp storage space.

Not every Wasaga Beach lot needs a full basement. On some, a frost wall with a slab, or a raft slab with ICF walls carried up, is the better answer, and we build both, along with full basements where the levels allow. Our gallery has a raft-slab build with ICF walls and gables carried to the roof. Cottage Builder Wasaga Beach — FAQs

Does Wasaga Beach require a grading plan for a new cottage?

Yes. For a new single-family dwelling the Town requires a lot grading and drainage plan stamped by a Certified Engineering Technologist or a Professional Engineer.

Do I need an NVCA permit to build near the water?

You may. The Town lists NVCA among the agencies whose permit or clearance may be required. NVCA requires new development to be outside flood-prone areas, and issues permits within 90 days of a complete application.

Why do levels change so much near the beach?

NVCA explains that in dynamic beach areas elevations change dramatically from season to season and year to year, which makes flood hazard zones unpredictable. When Georgian Bay is high, shoreline properties flood more readily.

Should a Wasaga Beach cottage have a basement?

It depends on the lot and the levels. Some suit a full ICF basement; others suit a frost wall and slab, or a raft slab with ICF walls carried up. We build all three.

Can a seasonal cottage become a year-round home later?

If it is built in ICF, the walls are already four-season. The change is in mechanical systems and finishes, not the structure.

Are you based in Wasaga Beach?

No. We are based in Barrie, and Wasaga Beach is one of the Simcoe County towns on our published service area.
