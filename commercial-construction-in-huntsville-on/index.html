Commercial construction in Huntsville ON starts with one permit

Anyone planning commercial construction in Huntsville ON runs into something most Ontario towns do not have. The Town of Huntsville has no zoning by-law and no stand-alone site plan by-law. The whole Town is designated a single Community Planning Permit Area under By-law 2022-97, and that one by-law decides what can be built, where, and on what conditions.

For a house, the by-law carves out an exemption. A new single detached dwelling that meets every standard and sits away from the shoreline, the waterfront and a short list of other features can go straight to a building permit. There is no matching exemption for a store, a shop, a warehouse or an office. Outside a few narrow cases, such as development already covered by a binding site plan agreement under the Town's former Site Plan Control By-law 2018-152, a new commercial building in Huntsville needs a Community Planning Permit. And section 1.4.2 is plain about the order: no building permit is issued for work that needs one until it has been granted.

One application instead of four

Huntsville's by-law describes, in its own overview, what the permit replaces. It combines minor variances, zoning amendments and site plan agreements into a single development application, and it can take the place of a site alteration permit and a tree removal permit as well. It does not require mandatory public meetings, and only the applicant has the right to appeal a decision.

What Huntsville asks in return is that you know which class of permit you are in. The by-law sets three, and the class decides who signs off.

The bottom box is the one that catches commercial owners. Table 1.2 says it twice: a use shall not be added as a permitted use through a Community Planning Permit. If what you want to build is not on the list for the precinct your lot is in, the conversation is no longer about classes at all.

The precinct decides what a Huntsville building may be

Huntsville's schedules divide the Town of Huntsville into precincts, and Section 4 of the by-law lists the permitted uses for each. Several are commercial or industrial: Regional Commercial, the Central Business District, Urban Mixed Use, Waterfront Commercial outside the settlement areas, and three Employment precincts, Urban Business Employment, Community and Rural Business Employment in Port Sydney, and Heavy Employment.

The lists are specific, and they do not always say what you would guess:

  • Regional Commercial allows retail stores, food stores, a home improvement centre, a motor vehicle dealership, restaurants, a shopping centre and tourist establishments. An office is permitted only as an accessory use to another permitted use.
  • Urban Business Employment allows a contractor's establishment, light industrial uses, a warehouse, self storage, a transportation depot and an office as principal uses. Accessory retail is capped at 200 square metres.

So a stand-alone office building on a Regional Commercial lot, or a contractor's yard on a Regional Commercial lot, which that precinct's list does not include, is not a matter of design. It is a by-law amendment, with its own timeline, before any permit class applies. Checking the precinct is the first thing to do with any Huntsville commercial lot, and it costs nothing but a look at the schedules.

What the Town can ask for, and what it can attach

Two appendices to the Huntsville by-law set the scope of a commercial application. Appendix A lists the studies the Town may require at the time of application, among them a functional servicing study, a stormwater management study, a traffic impact study, a salt management plan, a visual impact study, a tree inventory and preservation plan, and a geotechnical study including slope stability. Which ones apply is settled with the Town for each site.

Appendix B lists the conditions a Huntsville permit can carry. For commercial or industrial development it names access facilities such as ramps, curbs, turning lanes and traffic direction signs, and lighting under the Town's Outdoor Lighting By-law 2016-3, designed to limit the effect on lake views, night skies and natural features.

The Huntsville condition that matters most to us is in section 1.16.5.3. A permit may add conditions to minimize the extent of site grading, restrict site alteration in areas of steep and rocky terrain, preserve natural features, and minimize paved areas. On a Huntsville commercial site, that is a planning rule about the foundation. If the permit limits how much of the rock can be taken off, the building has to sit on the ground roughly as it is, and the foundation has to follow it.

That is the work we do most. Where there is exposed bedrock, footings are scribed and pinned to it rather than the site being levelled to suit the footing. Foundations step with the grade instead of fighting it. Our Footings on Exposed Bedrock project shows the method, and on Pebble Ridge the footing forms were laid out across broken rock for a large engineered package. On a site where the permit is protecting the terrain, a foundation that works with it is not just cheaper; it is what keeps the application in its class.

Two development charges, measured two ways

Huntsville is a lower-tier town in the District Municipality of Muskoka, and a commercial building pays development charges to both. The Huntsville charge and the Muskoka charge are calculated differently enough that it is worth setting them side by side.

The Huntsville charge is per square metre and pays for fire services, highways and development-related studies. The District's is per square foot, and most of it is water and wastewater: on its 2025 schedule a fully serviced building pays $11.41 a square foot, while an unserviced one pays $2.07, covering roads and rural septage. Whether the lot is on municipal water and sewer moves the District number by a factor of more than five.

Timing differs too. Huntsville collects at building permit issuance, at the rate in force when payment is due. The District's pamphlet says its charge is the one determined on the day of application for site plan control, or rezoning if that applies instead, and is otherwise due at permit issuance. Huntsville's single permit replaces both of those applications, so ask the District how that date is fixed for a Community Planning Permit before you rely on it. Two other District rules are worth knowing: enlarging an existing industrial building by less than 50 per cent of its floor area is exempt, and commercial accommodation, such as resort units, is charged per unit rather than per square foot.

Commercial Construction in Huntsville ON — FAQs

Does a commercial building in Huntsville need a Community Planning Permit?

In almost every case. By-law 2022-97 exempts a new single detached house that meets its standards, but there is no matching exemption for new commercial development, and no building permit is issued for work that needs a Community Planning Permit until it is granted.

Does Huntsville still have site plan control?

Not as a separate process. The Community Planning Permit combines minor variances, zoning amendments and site plan agreements into one application. Development covered by a still-valid agreement under the former Site Plan Control By-law 2018-152 is exempt.

Can a Community Planning Permit allow a use the precinct does not list?

No. The by-law says a use shall not be added as a permitted use through a permit; a use not listed for the precinct needs an amendment to the by-law.

What development charges apply to commercial space in Huntsville?

Two. The Town charges $45.57 per square metre of non-residential space as of January 1, 2026. The District of Muskoka's 2025 schedule charges $11.41 per square foot for fully serviced space and $2.07 for unserviced space. Both are indexed annually.

Can you build a foundation without blasting the site flat?

Often, yes. Where there is exposed bedrock we scribe and pin footings to it and step the foundation with the grade, which suits a permit that limits site alteration. The engineer and the site decide.

Are you based in Huntsville?

No. We are based in Barrie, and Huntsville is one of the Muskoka towns on our published service area.
