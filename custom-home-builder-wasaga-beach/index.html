The old building is part of the budget

Ask a custom home builder Wasaga Beach owners are talking to about cost, and most of the conversation will be about the new house. In Wasaga Beach, the house that is already on the lot belongs in that conversation too, because of one rule in the Town of Wasaga Beach's development charges.

Development charges are the per-house amounts a municipality collects to pay for the roads, pipes and services that growth needs. Wasaga Beach's current charges came into effect on May 24, 2026, under two by-laws: By-law 2026-35, which applies to every lot in town, and By-law 2026-36, which adds a charge in the Beach Area. The Town's pamphlet then sets out a redevelopment rule. A credit is given against the charges owing where a permit to demolish the existing building was obtained first, before it came down. The credit only applies if the building permit for the new house is issued within five years of the demolition permit.

Two conditions, then, and both are about order. The demolition permit comes before the demolition, not after. The new building permit comes within five years, not six. Miss either and the credit is gone.

We are DMT Building Group, based in Barrie, Ontario. Wasaga Beach is on the list of Simcoe County towns we publish as our service area, and we build custom homes in insulated concrete form (ICF) from the footings to the roofline, with our own crews doing the concrete.

From the old house to the occupancy permit

Lay a Wasaga Beach replacement build out on a line and the Town's rules fall at each end of it. The demolition starts one clock. The occupancy permit closes the other.

The middle of the line is where the money splits. The Town's pamphlet says its charge is the one determined under the by-laws, with applicable interest, on the date the first building permit is issued, unless a site plan or rezoning application came first. For a house that is not rental housing, though, the Town's charges are payable in full when an occupancy permit is issued or the building is first occupied. The County of Simcoe's charge works differently: its bulletin says the County charge is payable in full when the building permit is issued.

For a Wasaga Beach custom build that means two payments, months apart. One arrives as you are mobilising excavation. The larger one arrives as you are moving in. Plan the cash for each, and remember that the Town adjusts its rates every January 1, and that any charge left unpaid after it is due is added to the property's tax roll.

What the charges add up to on one house

For a single detached house, the Town of Wasaga Beach's townwide charge under By-law 2026-35 is $39,769. Roughly $28,300 of that is engineered services: roads, water, wastewater and storm drainage. The rest is general services such as recreation, parks, fire and the library. In the Beach Area mapped in the pamphlet, By-law 2026-36 adds $5,623 for Beach Area wastewater works. The County of Simcoe adds $14,975 for 2026.

Those totals leave out the two school boards, which also charge and which the Town's pamphlet directs you to contact directly. They also assume a full charge. On a replacement lot, the demolition credit is what can bring the Town's part down, and the Town's Building Department, which the pamphlet names as the first point of contact, is who confirms the figure for a given address.

We do not set or calculate any of these Wasaga Beach numbers. What we can do is put them on the table at the first site walk, so the cost of the house includes the cost of building it here.

The paperwork in between, in Wasaga Beach

The Town's Demolition Application Checklist is short: the building permit application form, a demolition checklist, a site plan showing every structure being removed, and confirmation that services have been capped, followed by a final inspection. Do that before any machine arrives on a Wasaga Beach lot and the credit stays available.

The Single Family Dwelling Application Checklist is longer, and several of its lines are worth reading as a builder. Alongside the construction drawings, site plan and legal survey, Wasaga Beach asks for an Energy Efficiency Design Summary, an HVAC design, a lot grading plan with its own checklist, a road occupation form, a water and sewer connection or a septic permit where applicable, a truss layout with stamped profiles, and a floor layout with stamped beam profiles. It notes that NVCA or MTO approval may be applicable.

The checklist also lists the inspections a new house goes through: footings, foundation, underground plumbing, framing, HVAC and plumbing rough-ins, insulation, occupancy and a final. The first two are ours. On a Wasaga Beach job we book our pours around them, because a wall cannot go up on a footing that has not been inspected.

At the very end of a Wasaga Beach build, the Town's Lot Grading Certification Check List comes back to the foundation. It confirms the final grades against the approved plan at the top of foundation wall and the underside of footing, that finished grade sits at least 150 mm below the brick or siding, that there is no settlement at or next to the foundation walls, and that the weeping tile sleeve elevation is confirmed where it applies. A foundation built to the plan's numbers is what lets that list be ticked.

Custom Home Builder Wasaga Beach — FAQs

Do I get a development charge credit for tearing down my old house in Wasaga Beach?

Possibly. The Town gives a credit where a permit to demolish was obtained first, before the demolition, and only if the building permit for the new house is issued within five years of the demolition permit.

What are the development charges on a new house in Wasaga Beach?

From May 24, 2026, the Town's townwide charge on a single detached house is $39,769, plus $5,623 in the Beach Area. The County of Simcoe charge is $14,975 for 2026. School boards charge separately.

When are development charges paid in Wasaga Beach?

The Town's residential charges, other than for rental housing, are payable in full when an occupancy permit is issued or the house is first occupied. The County's charge is payable when the building permit is issued.

What inspections does a new house in Wasaga Beach go through?

The Town's checklist lists footings, foundation, underground plumbing, framing, HVAC and plumbing rough-ins, insulation, occupancy and a final inspection.

Do you build subdivision homes in Wasaga Beach?

No. We build custom homes in ICF on your lot, from your plans, from the footings to the roofline.

Are you based in Wasaga Beach?

No. We are based in Barrie, and Wasaga Beach is one of the Simcoe County towns on our published service area.
