Footings on Bedrock

When the lot is solid rock, there is more than one way to build on it. We use the one that suits the site.

Overview

Cottage country sits on the Canadian Shield, and sooner or later every builder here meets a lot where the topsoil runs out and solid granite begins. Standard footing details assume a level excavated base; rock gives you anything but. It is work we do a lot of, and it is worth saying plainly that there is more than one right answer.

The two approaches we use most often are scribing the footing formwork to the rock — cutting each board to the stone's real profile and pinning the footing to it with epoxied dowels — or scribing the ICF itself directly onto the rock and pouring off that. Which one we recommend depends on the rock profile, the grade, the engineering and how much of the footprint is actually on stone.

Sometimes the answer is neither, and the right move is levelling, engineered fill or removing rock with our partners. We will tell you which it is after we have walked the site, rather than selling you the method we happen to like.

How we build on rock

Footings scribed to the stone

Every form board is cut to the rock's real profile so the footing bears fully on sound rock — no voids underneath, no concrete bleeding out under the forms.

Or ICF scribed straight to the rock

Where it suits the site we scribe the insulated concrete form itself onto the rock and pour off that, taking a step out of the sequence.

Pinned with epoxied dowels

Rebar dowels drilled and epoxy-set into sound rock tie the footing — and everything above it — to the Shield itself.

The best bearing surface there is

Sound bedrock has essentially nothing beneath it to settle. The challenge is purely in the forming and anchoring, which is the craft.

Stepped to follow the rock

Level runs connected by vertical steps, engineered so every run bears on stone and every top lands where the walls need it.

Honest about the alternatives

If levelling, engineered fill or rock removal is the better answer for your lot, we will say so and coordinate it.

What's Included — Our Process

Scope, spelled out. Here's what this service covers on a typical project:

  • Site walk and assessment of the exposed rock
  • A recommendation on method — scribed footings, ICF scribed to rock, or another approach entirely
  • Cleaning and preparation of the bearing surface
  • Formwork scribed board-by-board to the rock profile
  • Rebar dowels drilled and epoxy-anchored into sound rock
  • Engineered pour, stepped as the rock demands
  • Stepped insulated concrete walls to bring the build level

Footings on Bedrock — FAQs

Can standard footings be used on bedrock?

Not as drawn. Standard details assume a level, excavated base, whereas on exposed rock the bearing surface rises and falls. The forms have to be scribed to the stone and the footing stepped so it bears on sound rock everywhere — otherwise you get voids and point loads.

Is scribing the only way to build on rock?

No, and that is worth saying. We also scribe ICF directly onto the rock and pour off that, and on some lots the right answer is levelling, engineered fill or removing rock. The site, the grade and the engineering decide — we will recommend a method after walking it.

Is building on bedrock better than soil?

Sound bedrock is the best bearing surface you can build on — there is essentially nothing to settle. The difficulty is all in the forming and anchoring.

Do you blast or remove rock?

Wherever possible we work with the rock rather than against it, scribing and stepping to its contours. Where removal is genuinely required we coordinate it with trusted partners.

Does my cottage lot need this?

If you have exposed granite anywhere on the footprint, very likely yes. Send photos of the site with your quote request and we will tell you straight.
