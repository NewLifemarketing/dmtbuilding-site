Overview

An addition has to do two hard things at once: respect the house that's already there, and give you space that feels like it was always part of the plan. We build additions in ICF so the new structure is stronger, warmer and quieter than what it joins — and we manage the tie-in so the transition is seamless.

From bumping out a kitchen to adding a full second wing, DMT handles excavation, footings, foundation and walls as one accountable contractor, working around your household with as little disruption as possible.

Why ICF for Additions

No cold new room

Additions are notorious for being the coldest room in the house. A continuous R-22+ ICF wall ends that before it starts.

Clean structural tie-in

Concrete walls give the connection between old and new real rigidity — no flex, no cracking drywall at the joint.

Quieter by design

Concrete-and-foam walls keep street and yard noise out of the new space.

Weather-tough during the build

ICF stands up to the elements mid-construction better than open framing, which matters in an occupied home.

Additions — FAQs

Can an ICF addition attach to a conventionally framed house?

Yes. The connection details are engineered case-by-case, and it's one of the most common ways owners get their first taste of ICF performance.

Will we have to move out during construction?

Usually not. Most of the structural work happens outside the existing exterior wall, and we sequence the breakthrough for late in the schedule.

Do additions need their own foundation?

Yes — any structural addition needs proper footings and foundation. That's our core trade, including on difficult ground and exposed rock.

How do you match the addition to the existing house?

Exterior finishes go over ICF the same way they go over framing — siding, stone, brick or stucco — so the addition can match or deliberately contrast, your call.
