Two counters, in a fixed order

If you are comparing custom home builders Brampton has to offer, ask each of them one question: what happens before the permit? In Brampton the answer is longer than in most Ontario towns. The City requires architectural review and approval by its staff for every new custom home, whether single detached, semi-detached, duplex or freehold townhouse, and that includes a replacement house on an existing lot and small infill developments.

That review happens in the Planning Division, through BramPlanOnline. Only once it is done do you take the approved drawings to the Building Division and apply for the permit through the Brampton Building and Business Portal. Two departments, two portals, one after the other. Anyone who prices a Brampton custom home as if the permit were the first step has left a stage out of the schedule.

We are DMT Building Group. We are based in Barrie, Ontario, and we build across the Greater Toronto Area; Brampton is not one of the towns on our published list, so treat us as a Barrie builder who comes to your lot, not a local office. We build custom homes in insulated concrete form (ICF), with our own crews on the footings, the foundation and the walls. Most of this page is about the approvals that come before any of that, because in Brampton they decide where and how high the house sits.

What the Custom Home review looks at

The City's Custom Homes review covers two things: architectural control, and grading, servicing and site drainage. Urban Design staff review the elevations against Brampton's City-wide Urban Design Guidelines. Development Engineering staff review and approve the site servicing and grading plan. When both are done, Urban Design stamps the final elevations, and Development Engineering tells you how to obtain the approved engineering plans and pay the grading securities. The fee is $530 per unit under User Fee By-law 380-2003.

The submission list is short but specific. One digital copy each of:

  • A site plan, for location and building placement
  • All building elevations, with materials, roof slope and heights shown
  • All floor plans and a roof plan
  • A grading and servicing plan
  • The registered owner's authorization
  • Colour-rendered elevations, 3D renderings, or a finishing material sample board

All of it in metric, at a standard scale, with a title block, legend, key plan, north arrow and municipal address. One more item catches people: the site, grading and servicing plans must identify every tree over 30 cm in diameter at breast height that the work will affect, and which ones are being kept. A Tree Removal or Injury Permit from the City is needed before any tree on the property is damaged or removed.

The grading plan sets the foundation

For a builder who starts at the footing, the most important document in that first stage is not the elevations. It is the grading and drainage plan. Brampton's Custom Built Homes package says it must be prepared by a professional engineer or an Ontario Land Surveyor, approved by Development Engineering, and returned stamped, and the stamped plan goes into the building permit submission. It fixes the elevations the whole house is built to.

The City's own grading notes, attached to that package, set some hard numbers. The ground from the finished first floor down to the centreline of the road in front must fall at no less than 2% and no more than 6%. Driveways run between 2% and 8%. Lawns and swales slope between 2% and 6%, and a grade change of more than 1.0 metre takes a retaining wall. All surface drainage has to be contained on the lot and discharged where the City has approved, without making things worse for the neighbours.

Those numbers arrive on our desk as a top-of-foundation elevation. Our job is to land the footing and the top of the ICF wall exactly where the stamped plan puts them, so the 2% to 6% the City approved is the 2% to 6% that gets built. We build to the stamp, and if the ground on the day does not match the drawing, we flag it before anything is poured, not after.

Building on a teardown lot in Brampton

Many new home builders in Brampton work on lots that already have a house on them. If that is your situation, two Brampton rules shape the start of the project.

The first is who approves the demolition. Removing a residential building in Brampton is regulated by City By-law 203-99 and Section 33 of the Planning Act, and the permit for it is issued by City Council. It is applied for through the same Brampton Building and Business Portal, but it is a separate permit from the one for the new house, and it belongs early in the schedule.

The second is the money. A new single detached home in Brampton pays development charges before the building permit is released, and the City's current sheet, effective August 1, 2026, puts the total for a single or semi-detached unit at $155,650.32: $69,993.78 to the City of Brampton, $79,980.54 to the Region of Peel, and $4,376.00 and $1,300.00 to the two school boards. The rates are indexed every February 1 and August 1. The Custom Built Homes package notes that a credit may apply for dwelling units previously demolished on the property, or where a demolition permit has been issued and the demolition happens no later than four months after the building permit is issued. Confirm the details with the City's Finance Department before you count on it; on a figure that size, the timing of the old house coming down is worth planning around.

Infill also changes how the site is worked. Our Teardown & Rebuild project was exactly this: old structure out, then new engineered footings and foundation in, on a tight lot with houses a few metres away on both sides and spoil that had to leave the site. In an older Brampton neighbourhood the lot itself may also be inside the Mature Neighbourhood Area, where the City applies Site Plan Review to new or replacement single detached houses of 50 square metres or more. Zoning Services can tell you whether yours is.

Zoning, twice over

One more Brampton detail belongs in any 2026 conversation with home builders in Brampton. The City approved a new comprehensive Zoning By-law, 014-2026, on February 4, 2026, and it is under appeal at the Ontario Land Tribunal. Until those appeals are resolved, the City applies both the new by-law and the old one, 270-2004, and the most restrictive provision prevails. A custom home designed only to the old numbers can lose height, coverage or setback when the plans examiner checks the new ones. Have your designer check both before the elevations go to Urban Design, so the house is stamped once.

Custom Home Builders Brampton — FAQs

Do I need City approval before applying for a building permit?

Yes. Brampton requires Custom Home architectural control review and an approved grading and servicing plan before a custom home's building permit application is submitted. The fee is $530 per unit.

Who approves demolishing the existing house?

Brampton City Council issues residential demolition permits, under By-law 203-99 and Section 33 of the Planning Act. You apply through the Brampton Building and Business Portal.

How much are development charges on a new house in Brampton?

The City's sheet effective August 1, 2026 lists a total of $155,650.32 for a single or semi-detached unit, covering the City, the Region of Peel and both school boards. Rates are indexed each February 1 and August 1, and are paid before the permit is released.

Can I get credit for the house I am tearing down?

Possibly. The City's custom home package says a credit may apply for units previously demolished, or where demolition happens no later than four months after the building permit is issued. Confirm with the Finance Department.

Do I need a permit to remove trees on my lot?

Yes. Brampton requires a Tree Removal or Injury Permit before a tree on the property is injured or removed, and the custom home drawings must show affected trees over 30 cm in diameter.

Are you a Brampton company?

No. We are based in Barrie and build across the Greater Toronto Area. We travel to the lot for the site walk and the build.
