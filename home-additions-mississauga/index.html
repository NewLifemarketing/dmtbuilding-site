No site plan review, so the zoning numbers are the whole test

Here is the fact that shapes every addition in Mississauga. The City's Site Plan Control By-law 0293-2006 designates every piece of land inside the city as a site plan control area, then carves out one large exception: since the 2024 amendment, any residential development of 10 units or less does not need site plan approval. A house addition sits well inside that exception.

That sounds like good news, and mostly it is. There is no design panel to persuade. But it also means nobody negotiates with you. Your addition either fits the regulations written into Mississauga's Zoning By-law 0225-2007 or it needs a variance, and the building permit reviewers check it against those regulations line by line. On most Mississauga lots, three or four numbers decide the outcome of a home addition Mississauga reviewers see long before the floor plan gets interesting.

This page is about the part that comes before our crew arrives: working out what your lot will let you build.

The numbers that decide a Mississauga home addition

From Table 4.2.1 of the Zoning By-law, which covers detached houses on typical lots in the R1 to R5 zones. Your zone is on the City of Mississauga zoning map; the figures below are the base rules for each.

  • Lot coverage. The house may cover at most 25% of the lot in R1, 30% in R2, 35% in R3, and 40% in R4 and R5. A ground-floor addition spends this allowance directly; a second storey over the existing footprint spends none of it.
  • Rear yard. On an interior lot the minimum rear yard is 7.5 m in every one of those five zones. A rear addition stops where that line is.
  • Side yards that grow with height. In R2 the interior side yard is 1.8 m plus 0.61 m for each storey or part of a storey above the first; in R3 it is 1.2 m plus the same 0.61 m per storey. On a house addition Mississauga zoning adds side yard with every storey, so a second storey built straight up off a one-storey wall can land inside the required yard even though the wall below it is legal.
  • Height. The base maximum is 10.7 m across R1 to R5 in Mississauga.

That side-yard rule is the one that surprises people. It is why so many house additions Mississauga homeowners plan as a simple second storey end up with an upper floor stepped in from the side walls, or with a variance application before the permit.

Up or back: how the rules split the choice

Some of Mississauga's older neighbourhoods carry an extra layer, and it is the part of Mississauga zoning that most often catches an addition. Where an R1 exception zone says so (R1-1, R1-2 and R1-3 are examples in the by-law), Table 4.2.2's infill regulations apply on top of the base rules. Three of them bear directly on an addition:

  • A floor area cap. Gross floor area for infill residential is limited to 190 m² plus 0.20 times the lot area. On a 750 m² lot that works out to 340 m² for the whole house, existing and new together.
  • A depth limit. The maximum dwelling unit depth is 20.0 m, so a long rear addition on a deep lot can run out of depth before it runs out of yard.
  • Lower roofs. The highest ridge of a sloped roof is capped at 9.5 m on lots with 22.5 m of frontage or more and 9.0 m on narrower ones, a flat roof at 7.5 m, and the eaves at 6.4 m above average grade.

If the floor area cap is nearly used up by the house you already have, neither direction helps much, and that is worth knowing before you pay for drawings. Your designer can confirm the zone from the Mississauga online zoning by-law; we can tell you what the ground and the existing foundation allow once the envelope is known.

When the addition is really a second unit

The other question to settle early is who the new space is for. The City of Mississauga lets the owner of a detached, semi-detached or townhouse build up to two additional residential units on the property, and the City lists additions among the ways an attached unit can be created. That changes the job in three ways the City states plainly on its additional residential units page.

First, the City notes that development charges will also apply for additions and garden suites. Second, it flags that the Ontario Building Code has additional requirements for a building with more than two units, and that these may affect the design. Third, and in the owner's favour, the building permit fee for a permit to build or legalize additional units is refunded once the unit is built and its inspections are complete.

If you are thinking further ahead, toward a fourplex, read the Mississauga fourplex rules before you add a square metre. Under the fourplex by-law, a conversion has to fit all four units inside the footprint of the home as it existed on December 6, 2023. An addition built now does not enlarge that footprint for conversion purposes. A purpose-built fourplex is treated differently, with more height, reduced setbacks and more lot coverage, which is a new-build question rather than an addition.

What the City asks for when you apply

Every Mississauga building permit now goes in through ePlans, the City's online portal. The City of Mississauga checklist for a residential addition is specific, and a few items on it matter more for a concrete addition than for a framed one.

The site plan has to report site statistics: lot area and frontage, lot coverage in square metres and as a percentage, gross floor area broken down by floor, and dwelling unit depth. Those are exactly the zoning numbers above, so the reviewer can check them without measuring anything. The foundation and basement plan has to state the soil bearing capacity, the foundation wall and footing specifications, and where any step-down footings fall. The forms include the Applicable Law Form, an SB-12 energy efficiency design summary on the prescriptive path, Schedule 1 designer information, the Single Dwelling Unit Information Letter and a water service pipe sizing form.

The Applicable Law Form is where the approvals outside the Building Division surface, and in Mississauga there are three that can apply to a house:

Conservation authority

Most of Mississauga is in the Credit River watershed and Credit Valley Conservation's area; the City of Mississauga is one of its member municipalities. CVC may require its own permit for building near a watercourse, floodplain, valley slope, wetland, Lake Ontario or hazardous land, and its regulation mapping was updated on February 20, 2026. Toward the east of Mississauga, the Etobicoke and Mimico creek watersheds belong to TRCA.

Trees

On private property in Mississauga, injuring or removing any tree 15 cm or more in diameter needs a City permit, and the City says arborist reports are required for development and construction projects. Excavation for a rear addition is often what puts a mature tree's roots in play.

Heritage

Mississauga has three heritage conservation districts, Meadowvale Village, Old Port Credit Village and Streetsville, plus more than a thousand listed properties. Check before design if your Mississauga house is in one.

Mississauga home additions — FAQs

Does a house addition in Mississauga need site plan approval?

Generally no. The City's Site Plan Control By-law 0293-2006 exempts residential development of 10 units or less. You still need a building permit, and the addition must meet the Zoning By-law.

Can I build a second storey over my bungalow?

Often, but check the side yards. In R2 and R3 zones the minimum interior side yard grows by 0.61 m for each storey above one, so a second storey may need to step in from the walls below.

Is there a limit on how big my house can get?

Every zone limits lot coverage, from 25% in R1 to 40% in R4 and R5. Where the R1 infill exception applies, gross floor area is also capped at 190 m² plus 0.20 times the lot area, and dwelling depth at 20.0 m.

Do development charges apply to an addition?

They can. The City's additional residential units page says development charges will also apply for additions and garden suites. Confirm with the City for your specific project.

Do I need a tree permit to build an addition?

If the work will injure or remove a private tree 15 cm or more in diameter, yes. The City requires arborist reports for development and construction projects.

Are you based in Mississauga?

No. We are based in Barrie and build across the Greater Toronto Area, including Mississauga.
