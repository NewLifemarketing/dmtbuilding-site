One town, four development charge rates

Ask a Midland custom home builder what a new house costs and the honest first answer is: which street? The Town of Midland's development charge by-law, 2025-21, was passed on March 19, 2025 as a town-wide and area-specific by-law. That second half is the part that surprises people. Two lots a few kilometres apart, both inside Midland, can carry very different charges before a single block goes up.

Here is how the Town's 2026 indexed schedules break down for a single or semi-detached dwelling:

  • Town-wide services, everywhere in Midland: $18,804 (roads, fire, police, parks and recreation, library and growth studies)
  • Urban serviced area: an extra $8,880 for water ($3,410) and wastewater ($5,470), for a town total of $27,684
  • Tiffin by the Bay: a further $2,600 area-specific charge for services related to a highway
  • Balm Beach Road: a further $3,201 area-specific charge for water and wastewater

The County of Simcoe charges on top of all of that. Its By-law 6930 imposes residential development charges on all lands in the County, and for a single detached house in 2026 the County figure is $14,975, payable in full when the building permit is issued. The Town collects its own charges at the building permit too.

We are DMT Building Group, based in Barrie, Ontario. Midland is on the list of Simcoe County towns we publish as our service area, and we build custom homes there in insulated concrete form (ICF), with our own crews doing the concrete from the footings up. We do not set these charges and cannot change them. We can make sure they are in your budget before the design is, not after.

What the charges add up to, by part of Midland

The spread between the first bar and the last is more than $12,000, on the same house. That is real money on a custom build, and it is fixed by where the lot is, not by anything you or a builder decide.

There is a history behind it. The preamble to Midland's Zoning By-law 2004-90 records that, through County restructuring, lands formerly in the Townships of Tay and Tiny were transferred to the Town of Midland. The charge schedules split along similar lines: water and wastewater are charged in the urban serviced area, and the two named areas carry their own infrastructure costs.

On Midland Bay, a contour line draws the building envelope

The second thing that sets a Midland lot apart is the water, and here the Zoning By-law is unusually exact. In the R5 residential zone, section 5.5.3.7 says that on waterfront properties on Midland Bay, no building or structure may be within 15.0 metres of the 178.0 metre elevation adjacent to Georgian Bay. On top of that, every opening in a building on a waterfront lot has to sit no lower than 178.5 metres. Both elevations are measured on the Geodetic Surveys of Canada datum, NAD 1983.

So the envelope is not drawn from the property line or from wherever the water happens to be this summer. It is drawn from a surveyed contour, and the lowest window and door are pinned to a second one half a metre higher. Boathouses, pump houses, boat slips, docks and flood or erosion control works are exempt, and decks and stairs attached to the house may encroach as far as the zone's rear yard rule allows.

Exceptions matter here as well. Midland's zone codes carry site-specific exceptions, written as a suffix like R5-6, and those can be stricter. The exception at 447 Mundy's Bay Road, for example, sets a 20 metre shoreline vegetated buffer and a 25 metre dwelling setback from the high water mark. Check the exact zone code for your lot on the Town's online Zoning Tool before you fall for a building site.

For the foundation, the 178.5 metre rule sets a floor you design up to. A walkout facing the bay is still possible, as long as its openings clear the line. A stepped ICF foundation that follows a sloping waterfront grade is the kind of work our Lakeside ICF Build project story walks through.

From Zoning Certificate to inspection: the Midland permit

Midland's process starts with zoning. The Town offers a Planning First application for a Zoning Certificate, free of charge, to confirm that a proposed project complies with the zoning on the property, and its building page is plain that zoning compliance is required even where no building permit is.

For a new house, a lot grading drawing is required with the building permit application. The Town asks for an individual lot grading plan prepared by a Professional Engineer, an Ontario Land Surveyor or a CET, reviewed and approved before the permit is issued, with a lot grading deposit returned once the grading is inspected and the permit can be closed. Applications go through Cloudpermit.

Then comes the part that shapes a construction schedule. Midland books same-day inspections for requests entered into Cloudpermit, or phoned in, by 11 a.m., and inspects between 1 and 4 p.m. Inspectors cannot give an advance arrival time, and if the owner or agent is not there, re-inspection fees may apply.

On a concrete job, that window is worth knowing before the schedule is set, because an inspection the work depends on cannot be booked for a time of your choosing. For what a pour itself involves, what happens on an ICF pour day walks through it.

Midland Custom Home Builder — FAQs

What are the development charges on a new house in Midland?

For 2026, the Town's charge on a single detached house is $18,804 town-wide, or $27,684 in the urban serviced area. Tiffin by the Bay adds $2,600 and Balm Beach Road adds $3,201. The County of Simcoe adds $14,975.

When are development charges paid in Midland?

The Town collects its charges at the time of the building permit, and the County's charge is payable in full when the building permit is issued.

How close to Midland Bay can I build?

In the R5 zone, no building or structure may be within 15.0 metres of the 178.0 metre elevation, and openings must be at or above 178.5 metres. Site-specific exceptions can be stricter, so check your lot's zone code.

Do I need a lot grading plan for a new house in Midland?

Yes. The Town requires an individual lot grading plan by a Professional Engineer, Ontario Land Surveyor or CET, approved before the building permit is issued, plus a deposit.

How do building inspections work in Midland?

Requests made through Cloudpermit or by phone before 11 a.m. are inspected the same day, between 1 and 4 p.m. Inspectors cannot give an arrival time.

Are you based in Midland?

No. We are based in Barrie, and Midland is one of the Simcoe County towns on our published service area.
